# Independent Review

## 1. Main claim and strongest reconstruction

**Main claim:** Grammaticality is the status of a token relative to a socially sustained norm whose function is to relate signs, exponents, or values inside recurrent constructions in a scoped repertoire—and this role-based criterion, combined with a strict separation of five explanatory levels, explains why some interpretable deviations count as ungrammatical while others count as anomalous, ill-chosen, misspelled, or socially unfitting.

**Strongest charitable reconstruction:**

(a) *Motivation.* Interpretable deviations receive different public classifications (*\{le hiver}, *\{goed}, *\{depend of} vs. anomaly, collocation failure, spelling, register). Neither meaninglessness nor expressive substance marks the boundary (§1).

(b) *Level separation.* Classification talk conflates six distinct questions—status constitution, token application, semantic/pragmatic consequence, target attribution, response causation, epistemic justification—borne by five distinct objects (§2, Table 1). Source-ambiguity findings (Hofmeister et al.; Leivada & Westergaard) show ratings underdetermine causes; hence judgments cannot be truth-makers.

(c) *Constitutive answer.* Within a broad constructionist inventory (Goldberg; Sag), what makes a norm specifically *grammatical* is its role: relating already-individuated signs/exponents/values within recurrent constructions. Lexical conventions establish sign identity; phonotactics constrains shape; orthography represents expressions; usage norms govern deployment (§3, Table 2).

(d) *Normativity without response.* Recurrent reliance—"conformity to the relation is part of what counts, within the practice, as successfully tokening that construction"—supplies standards that responses track fallibly rather than constitute (§3.1). Miller's local performance standards are a precedent; "constitutive reliance" bridges their social sustenance and normativity.

(e) *Individuation without labels.* Candidate signs, constructions, and mixed analyses are supported by recurrence, opposition, substitution, omission, productivity, and held-out predictive distribution—not by metalinguistic labels; indeterminate grain stays indeterminate (§3.2).

(f) *Token failures sorted.* Only internal value incompatibility is grammatical per se; external misapplication and message-selection error preserve grammaticality (§4).

(g) *Semantic/pragmatic theories located.* Repairability (Abrusán) explains composition-internal classification; discourse-consequence profiles describe downstream effects of grammatical choices without defining membership (§5).

(h) *Testable consequences.* Assembly-role profiles estimated blind should predict spontaneous assembly-directed attribution in held-out cases; discourse-consequence profiles should predict repair-locus differences between value conflicts and realization failures (§6–7). Defeat conditions are explicit.

The argument's logical order—from objects, through individuation, to evidence—is coherent and honestly conditional throughout.

## 2. Desk impressions

**Ten seconds:** Dense, carefully structured theoretical linguistics/philosophy-of-language manuscript; terminology-heavy but disciplined; the AI disclosure, explicit defeat conditions, and constructed-stimulus caveats signal unusual methodological self-awareness. Abstract is too compressed to be parseable on first read. No figures beyond a schematic table-as-figure.

**Sixty seconds:** A reader will come away with three things: (i) a usable six-way disambiguation of "grammaticality" talk that inoculates acceptability research against category errors; (ii) a functional-role criterion that places disputed phenomena (elision, inflection, collocation, honorifics, orthography) without appealing to the very labels being explained; and (iii) a research program—independent profiling followed by held-out response measures—that tells experimentalists how to test the ontology rather than assume it. Whether the criterion succeeds is left partly open by design; the reader must decide whether that honesty is rigor or evasion.

## 3. Three strongest contributions

**C1 — The six-question architecture (§1–§2, Table 1, Fig. 1).** The separation of "Status constitution… Token application… Semantic or pragmatic consequence… Target attribution… Response causation… Epistemic justification," borne by five bearers, is the paper's most immediately useful contribution. The insistence that token-level response "is not constitutive of status" while diachronic feedback maintains norms gives a principled answer to why a judgment can be *wrong* rather than merely outvoted—a point many usage-based accounts cannot make.

**C2 — Constitutive reliance as the practice-normativity bridge (§3.1).** The move from Miller's community-relative local performance standards—which the author correctly notes leave normativity unexplained—to "constitutive reliance" is the paper's sharpest philosophical advance: participants rely on the relation in producing and interpreting tokens *as instances of that construction*, so conformity becomes a condition of token identity, not a statistical summary. This simultaneously blocks frequency-based reductionism and preserves response-independence.

**C3 — The value-error trichotomy (§4).** Distinguishing internal value incompatibility, external value misapplication, and message-selection error—and deriving from the constitutive account that "only internal incompatibility establishes a grammatical fault by itself"—is exactly the kind of derivation the selective-classification problem demands. It also generates the paper's most concrete empirical hypothesis: repair-locus differences between value conflicts and value-preserving realization failures, scaled by discourse-consequence profiles.

(Honorable mention: §6–7's explicit defeat conditions—"the constitutive account fails if its functional roles can't place… without relying on the responses to be explained"—set a standard the field rarely meets.)

## 4. Three strongest objections

**O1 (Conceptual defect): Residual circularity in the counterfactual test.** The four-part justification (§3.1) turns on holding fixed "lexical identities, the intended constructional values, and the situation" and asking whether changing the candidate relation changes "whether the material instantiates the recurrent construction." But *instantiation of a construction* is itself a notion whose boundaries are part of what the theory is supposed to ground. §3.2 supplies label-independent individuation criteria (recurrence, opposition, substitution), but the test in §3.1 never explicitly routes through them, so a skeptic can charge that the analysis of *\{le hiver} as article-realization failure presupposes that article realization is a construction-internal relation—the classification it purports to derive. *What would answer:* rewrite the test so that each input (sign identity, conventional value, site, scope) is certified by the §3.2 procedures *before* the counterfactual is run, and demonstrate this routing on one case end-to-end (see O2). If the routing cannot be completed for the motivating case, the objection stands.

**O2 (Evidential gap): No profile is ever executed.** The paper's own epistemological standard requires estimating an assembly-role profile from positive evidence before examining responses. Yet no case receives that treatment. The French analysis cites Boersma and Simonenko & Carlier rather than deriving elision's construction specificity; the Amele stimuli are frankly flagged as "constructed from Roberts's paradigm and haven't been validated"; *\{depend of} and *\{heavy rain} rest on informal distributional observations. The two "held-out predictions" remain promissory notes. As it stands, the constitutive claim floats above the very evidential discipline the paper prescribes for everyone else. *What would answer:* one complete worked profile—for instance, French elision: inventory of eliding/non-eliding function words, conditioning environments, medium variation, h muet/h aspiré contrasts—with an explicit held-out prediction about attribution, even at pilot scale. Absent this, the paper is a framework plus a promissory methodology.

**O3 (Conceptual defect / exposition): The exclusion of pure phonotactics risks definitional gerrymandering.** Table 2 and §3.1 exclude shape-only constraints from "grammar in the target sense." But the target sense—"public repertoire status for conventional fault attribution and descriptive grammar"—is asserted, not argued for, and it sits uneasily with both traditions and folk usage: generative grammar canonically includes phonology as a grammatical component (Chomsky & Halle 1968; verification required), and naive respondents plausibly report *\{bnick}-type strings as "not English words" i.e., grammar-directed. If the target notion is tailored so that the motivating cases land where the author wants them, the role criterion risks predicting the classifications by construction. The paper anticipates this ("the surface string does not decide the issue"), but anticipation is not argument. *What would answer:* (i) an independent motivation for excluding shape-only norms from *this* target—one that does not presuppose the assembly-role criterion; (ii) an explicit statement of what the account predicts about *attribution* for phonotactic violations (presumably sound-shape-directed, not grammar-directed), which is an empirical bet worth making visible.

**Source claims needing verification** (flagged, since I cannot establish them directly from the page): Roberts (1987) pp. 239–240, 298–300, 371 for the SS/DS paradigm and harmony facts; Simonenko & Carlier (2020) on h muet/elision; Bary (2025) and Simons (2025) on discourse-state pluralism; the Miller (2021) characterization of local performance standards. The manuscript's own hedging on Roberts is exemplary and should be retained after validation.

## 5. Testing central and boundary cases

***\{le hiver}.** The intended definite-masculine-singular value is recoverable; the candidate relation is conditioned realization of the article construction; pure phonotactics and graphic convention are the neighbors. *Classification: largely assumed.* The decisive evidence—"construction specificity, medium, lexical grouping, and elision distribution" (Table 5)—is enumerated but never assembled. The paper concedes this conditionally ("counts as a grammatical-form error **only if** that constructional relation… is what fails"), which is honest but leaves the flagship case undischarged.

***\{goed}.** Best-derived case. The productive past-tense construction supplies the assembly relation; the lexeme-specific pre-empted form \{went} supplies the lexical layer; hence mixed attribution. The classification follows from independently motivated properties (productivity, item restriction). One pressure point: the paper asserts the item-specific restriction is "partly lexical," but a skeptic could argue pre-emption is itself a paradigm-internal phenomenon; the mixed verdict depends on grain decisions §3.2 licenses but does not execute here.

**Pure phonotactic violation (e.g., a hypothetical \{bnick}).** *Classification: stipulated, and arguably mistargeted.* By the role criterion, a shape-only constraint is "phonological on this account," not grammatical. Yet the paper's own selective-classification datum—that interpretable deviations get called ungrammatical—has its clearest non-semantic exemplars here, and ordinary speakers' attributions likely run contrary to the account's placement. This is the boundary case the ontology handles least convincingly (see O3).

**Register/honorific mismatch vs. Japanese polite/plain style.** The account distinguishes deployment failure (assembly succeeded, occasion unfit) from grammaticalized constructional choice (domain, alternatives, conditioned selection). *Classification: underdetermined.* The deciding criteria—recurrence, alternative set, conditioned selection—are satisfied by keigo-style systems to a degree that arguably crosses the paper's own threshold; conversely, English register choice satisfies weaker versions. The paper acknowledges the diachronic slide but provides no procedure for fixing the boundary at a time. The risk is post hoc: whichever way respondents classify, the analyst can locate the system on the sliding scale.

**Misspelling vs. graphic realization of grammatical value.** Cleanly handled in principle: the medium doesn't determine norm type; the relation's role does. The case is well-chosen adversarial material, though again unexecuted—no actual writing-system example (e.g., a script where a graphic contrast realizes a morphosyntactic value) is developed.

**Amele SS/DS.** Correctly quarantined as a "source-grounded template." Notably, the paper derives rather than assumes the key dependency: the conflict reading holds "only if the subject information is linguistically represented in the clause chain," and a mere intention mismatch is reclassified as message-selection error. Good—but everything downstream waits on validation.

**Summary of assumption-vs-derivation:** *\{goed}* derived; Amele conflict condition derived (conditional on validation); *\{le hiver}* enumerated-but-not-executed; phonotactics stipulated; honorific/register boundary underdetermined.

## 6. Strongest rival account and a discriminating observation

The strongest named rival is **Boye & Harder's conventional-ancillarity / discursive-secondary-status account**: grammatical elements are those conventionally unable to carry focal attention or addressability. It shares the paper's anti-substantialism and anti-frequentialism and comes with ready-made diagnostics. The manuscript identifies the right pressure point—cross-classification where "constructionally organized material remains independently focusable"—but leaves it abstract, and candidly concedes discursive status "may prove a better general account."

An unnamed but serious rival is a **detectability/pre-emption model**: deviations are attributed to grammar just in case the repertoire supplies a determinate paradigmatic alternative, regardless of the failed relation's role. This predicts the grammar classification for *\{goed} (alternative: \{went}), *\{le hiver} (alternative: \{l'}), and *\{depend of} (alternative: \{on})—i.e., everything the assembly account gets right—while needing no ontology of roles.

**Discriminating observation:** find (or construct corpus evidence for) a case with a clear licensed alternative that respondents nonetheless attribute to *wording/style* rather than grammar—item-restricted collocations are the natural candidates (\{strong rain}? \{perform a crime}?). If attribution there diverges from alternative-availability, detectability alone fails and role does real work. Conversely, to discriminate against Boye & Harder: identify material that is high in assembly organization but independently focusable or addressable (e.g., an information-structurally active construction whose internal agreement or ordering relations are nonetheless obligatory)—if such cases are robustly grammar-attributed, ancillarity misclassifies them. The paper gestures at both quadrants; supplying one concrete instance of each, even impressionistically documented, would transform the comparison section from a draw into a contest.

## 7. Ranked revisions

1. **Execute one assembly-role profile end-to-end** (French elision is ideal: rich literature, sharp scope, available held-out contrast with h aspiré and with non-eliding contexts). Show the estimate, its components, and one held-out attribution prediction. This converts O2 from fatal to fixable and demonstrates that the §6 methodology is performable.
2. **Repair the counterfactual test's circularity exposure (O1)** by rewriting §3.1 so every input to the test is certified by the §3.2 individuation procedures before the test runs, and trace that certification explicitly for *\{le hiver}* and *\{goed}*.
3. **Argue for, don't assert, the target sense that excludes pure phonotactics (O3)**: give an independent characterization of "fault-attribution and descriptive-grammar grammaticality," state the account's prediction about phonotactic-violation attributions, and confront the generative-tradition inclusion of phonology in grammar.
4. **Replace the abstract concession to Boye & Harder with one worked cross-classification candidate**, and add the detectability/pre-emption model as a second rival with a proposed discriminating observation (§6 above).
5. **Tighten the gradience/indeterminacy machinery** so the theory cannot absorb any recalcitrant case by declaring layered roles or unsettled repertoire: specify in advance what pattern of evidence *forbids* a mixed or indeterminate verdict for a given case type.

## 8. Verdict

**MINOR CONCEPTUAL REVISION**

(with reservations bordering on major). The level architecture, the constitutive-reliance bridge, and the value-error trichotomy are genuine contributions, and the paper's explicitness about defeat conditions is exemplary. But the constitutive claim currently rests on a counterfactual test with a residual circularity exposure, a stipulated exclusion of phonotactics that shapes the headline results, and zero executed applications of the paper's own evidential standard. All three are repairable within one revision cycle—if the author performs, rather than promises, at least one full analysis. Should revisions 1–3 fail, the verdict would downgrade to MAJOR CONCEPTUAL REVISION, with the fallback position the author himself names: "a narrower theory of metalinguistic attribution."
