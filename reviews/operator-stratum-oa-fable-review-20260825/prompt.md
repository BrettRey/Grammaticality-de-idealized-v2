# Independent review brief

You are reviewing the manuscript supplied after this brief for possible submission
to *Linguistics and Philosophy*. You have not seen earlier drafts, reviews, plans,
or discussion. Read the complete manuscript and assess the argument that is actually
on the page. Do not edit files or propose line-level polishing.

Produce a self-contained review with these parts:

1. A one-sentence statement of the paper's main claim, followed by the strongest
   charitable reconstruction of its argument.
2. A ten-second desk-editor impression and a sixty-second account of what a reader
   will think the paper contributes.
3. The three strongest contributions, located by section and short quoted phrase.
4. The three strongest objections. Distinguish conceptual defects, evidential gaps,
   source claims needing verification, and exposition problems. Explain what would
   answer each objection.
5. Test at least four of the manuscript's central or boundary cases against its own
   account. Identify any case whose classification is assumed rather than derived.
6. Identify the strongest rival account or countermodel and an observation that
   would distinguish it from this paper's account.
7. Give a ranked list of at most five revisions. Prioritize changes to the argument;
   omit cosmetic and submission-formatting advice.
8. End with one verdict: READY TO SUBMIT, MINOR CONCEPTUAL REVISION, MAJOR
   CONCEPTUAL REVISION, or RECONSTRUCT.

Apply Rapoport's Rules: demonstrate understanding and points of agreement before
criticizing. Be charitable but exacting. Do not treat agreement with the author as
evidence. If you invoke literature beyond the manuscript, name it and mark the claim
as requiring verification unless you can establish it directly.
