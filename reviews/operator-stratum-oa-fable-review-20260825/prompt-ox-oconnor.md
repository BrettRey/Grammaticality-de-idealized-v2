# Cailin O'Connor-style philosophical review brief

Review the manuscript supplied after this brief from an analytical perspective
modelled on Cailin O'Connor's published work in philosophy of science, social
epistemology, conventions, evolutionary game theory, and the emergence and
maintenance of social norms. This is a simulated scholarly perspective, not a
claim that you are O'Connor or can report her private views.

The manuscript is being considered for *Linguistics and Philosophy*. Read the
complete text. Do not read or infer other reviews, and do not edit files.

Concentrate on these questions:

1. Does recurrent social reliance generate a correctness standard, or has the
   paper redescribed stable coordination as normativity?
2. What population structure, interaction process, expectations, sanctions, or
   historical dynamics would have to sustain the proposed public,
   repertoire-relative grammatical norms?
3. Can such norms be response-independent at token level while historically
   constituted by practice and response? Is the manuscript's synchronic/diachronic
   distinction adequate?
4. Does the account handle plural, contested, changing, and partially shared
   repertoires without appealing to an unrealistically unified community?
5. Does construction-internal assembly genuinely distinguish grammatical norms
   from phonological, lexical, orthographic, pragmatic, and social norms?
6. Which claims are constitutive proposals, which are causal explanations, and
   which are empirical conjectures? Identify any unsupported transitions.

Begin with the strongest charitable reconstruction and points of agreement. Then
give the strongest objections, test concrete cases from the manuscript, and state
what revisions or evidence would answer them. Locate findings by section and short
quoted phrase. Avoid line editing and cosmetic advice. Flag literature claims that
require source verification. End with a verdict: READY TO SUBMIT, MINOR CONCEPTUAL
REVISION, MAJOR CONCEPTUAL REVISION, or RECONSTRUCT.
