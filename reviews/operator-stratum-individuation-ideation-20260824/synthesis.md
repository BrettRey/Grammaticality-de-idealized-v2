## Convergence

Both models proposed four useful constraints on the grain of analysis:

1. candidate signs have to recur outside the disputed relation, while bound
   exponents can be identified through paradigmatic opposition;
2. a relational analysis needs substitution or contrast over an independently
   motivated site or class;
3. decomposition has to predict behaviour beyond the case used to posit it;
4. unresolved or changing cases need an indeterminate outcome rather than an
   automatically mixed or partly grammatical classification.

Both used *depend on* versus *heavy rain* to separate an item-restricted
constructional chain from a collocational overlay and supplied conditions under
which the initial analyses should reverse.

## Rejected proposals

- Fable's requirement that two independent patterns converge on every relevant
  class is too strong as an individuation rule and doesn't reliably exclude
  phonotactics: phonological natural classes can support several independent
  patterns. Its treatment of individual competence as an estimate of a public
  norm was also too externalist for the paper's pluralist scope.
- Ox Alpha made convergent, link-directed repair one of four individuation
  conditions. That reuses the response outcome the paper has to withhold and
  would restore the central circularity. Its claims that categoricality and
  stranding distinguish grammar from collocation were also stronger than the
  evidence licenses.

## Implemented adjudication

The manuscript now treats recurrence, paradigmatic opposition, substitution,
structural separability, and predictive surplus as defeasible evidence for the
grain of a candidate relation. No single diagnostic is necessary. A mixed
analysis is licensed only when each recovered relation is independently attested
and makes a distinct counterfactual prediction. Corrections, repairs, and grammar
labels remain held-out response evidence. The paper also distinguishes epistemic
underdetermination from a repertoire that is itself unsettled and allows either to
produce an indeterminate result.
