## Argument map

Grammaticality, the paper argues, conflates four separable questions: **coverage** (some analysis exists), **compatibility** (operator instructions cohere), **saturation** (contextually obligatory contrasts are supplied), and **licensing** (the community establishes the constructions). A grammaticality claim therefore compresses a population profile plus an individual's evidence-based estimate; felt anomaly and confidence are a separate read-out. Following Goodman, profiles earn their standing by **projection**: predicting repair, exposure response, transmission, and change. A dynamics module distinguishes evidence updating (precision) from adoption/retention (population change); absences diagnose non-licensing only given genuine opportunity plus a winning competitor (preemption) or divided evidence (winnerless cells); moribund contrasts lose evidence concentration before categorical status shifts. Opening cases sort accordingly: (1a) coverage failure; (1b) compatible but implausible; (1c) incompatible; (1f) unlicensed; left-branch extraction preempted; Russian *pobedit′* winnerless; centre embedding merely costly. Simulations serve as existence checks: adoption thresholds, not evidence precision alone, generate separated populations. Predictions cover concentration-ranked framing/satiation effects, L2 preemption transfer, concentration-loss precursors, operator-directed repair, and re-licensing signatures. (~155 words)

## Gate 1: Specialist terms used before earned or explained

**Classification: MINOR**

1. **MINOR** — p. 7, §3.6: "A preempted gap may elicit a confident 'that's impossible'. A winnerless cell may elicit an equally low rating…" Both terms carry the model's key diagnostic contrast but are used analytically here, roughly eight pages before either is defined (§4.5, p. 11; §5.5, pp. 14–15). A cold reader can reverse-engineer them from context, but this is the passage where the read-out typology is first motivated.
2. **MINOR** — p. 13, §5.3: "failed unification between grammatically encoded temporal contributions" and "failed unification among partial operator assignments." *Unification* is a term of art from unification-based formalisms, never introduced; yet compatibility is partly defined through it ("treats operator-value conflict as failed unification," p. 13). The intended precision level is unclear: metaphor or formal operation?
3. **MINOR** — p. 11, §4.3: "evidence-only control," "coupled runs," "interior population share," "numerical continuation of the full expected update" arrive in quick succession with only inline glosses. Each is decipherable, but the density peaks here relative to the rest of the manuscript.
4. **MINOR** — p. 12, §4.5 heading: "Actuation and apparent categorical exclusions." *Actuation* is standard historical-linguistics vocabulary but is never glossed, despite being a term with a contested theoretical history (Weinreich–Labov–Herzog) that the paper quietly repurposes.

No term is used in a way that blocks first-pass comprehension outright; definitions eventually arrive and are generally placed responsibly (e.g., *valeur* glossed at §3.2, p. 4; *evidence concentration* defined at point of use, §3.4, p. 6; *niche* flagged as defined in §4.1).

## Gate 2: Insufficiently grounded linguistic judgments or contextual restrictions

**Classification: MINOR**

1. **MINOR** — p. 5, §3.3: "Turkish suffix-harmony errors can have the same shape: the plural or past value remains identifiable, while the chosen allomorph isn't licensed in that phonological environment." Asserted with no citation, no example, and no pointer (Supplementary File 2 is not named until p. 18). This is an empirical claim about Turkish doing real classificatory work in the taxonomy.
2. **MINOR** — p. 14, §5.5: the *sheeps* case. The claims that regularization is unacceptable "without a metaphorical or playful justification" and that *the black sheeps of the family* "might signal a figurative usage" rest on the author's judgment alone, with no corpus or citation, in a section otherwise careful about evidence streams.
3. **MINOR** — p. 7, §3.6: "Centre embedding can feel very bad even when the speaker accepts the construction once its parse is made clear." Plausible, but offered as established phenomenology without support, in a paragraph that elsewhere frames comparable claims as predictions.

Everything else in this class is adequately sourced: the Danish results (p. 2), multiple modals (p. 11), Franjieh et al.'s North Ambrym figures (p. 10), the Verkerk et al. universal counts (p. 19), the COCA null result is explicitly flagged as an unregistered exploration (pp. 15, 24), and the Russian and *amn't* cases carry citations (p. 15). None of the three findings above prevents evaluation of a central claim; each marks a spot where the paper's own evidential discipline is stricter elsewhere.

## Gate 3: Premise-to-conclusion links unevaluable without the supplement

**Classification: MINOR**

1. **MINOR** — p. 11, §4.3: the simulation results ("at the calibrated scale," threshold vs. linear adoption, continuation analysis with two stable states, finite-population recovery) depend on calibration targets, parameter values, and the update rule, none of which appear in the main text. The paper mitigates this deliberately and repeatedly: results are labelled model-internal existence checks, the failed early-warning check is reported against interest, and the limits are stated (pp. 11, 22–24). A reader can therefore assess what the simulations are claimed to establish, but cannot check that they establish it.
2. **MINOR** — pp. 17–18, §6.1 (Change by re-licensing): the contrasting distributional signatures (bimodal-across-communities/categorical-within-speakers vs. within-speaker gradience with an S-curve) are the paper's sharpest discriminating prediction, yet follow from dynamical structure derived only in the supplement. The qualitative logic (exact compatibility cannot be entrenched away; a distinct construction enters the repertoire) is stated and is evaluable as far as it goes.

Both findings concern the deliberately quarantined formal layer. The core architecture (four conditions, profile/estimate/read-out splits, opportunity-based diagnosis of absence) is argued entirely in ordinary language and is evaluable standalone, so nothing here blocks evaluation of a central claim.

## Gate 4: Falsifiers whose intended target is unclear

**Classification: NONE**

Every falsifier names its target precisely: the public-update-role boundary (§3.2, p. 5), the multipart profile vs. a single latent score (§3.5, p. 6), obligatoriness trajectories (§4.5, p. 11), the preempted-gap diagnosis of left-branch extraction with its three counted failure conditions (§5.5, p. 15), the concentration rank order with its reversal condition (§6.1, p. 17), L2 transfer (p. 17), the concentration-loss precursor with timing of dispersion declared orthogonal (pp. 16–17), the both-licensed dative control (p. 18), repair-type/controller claims (p. 18), loss-vs-recovery boundaries (p. 18), and intonational operators (p. 18). Several predictions are explicitly downgraded or withdrawn where the design does not support them (early warning, p. 11; dispersion timing, p. 17), which is the opposite of unclear targeting.

GATE: PASS
