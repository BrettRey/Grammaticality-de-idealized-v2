## Argument map

**Problem (§1–2, pp. 2–3).** A single grammaticality variable runs together four questions (coverage, compatibility, saturation, licensing) plus processing/plausibility.

**Theoretical proposal (§3–4, pp. 3–11).** OVMG: a state theory (four conditions, population profile vs. speaker estimate vs. analyst estimate, "evidence concentration", anomaly/confidence read-out) and a dynamics theory (opportunity, candidates, outside option; adoption/retention; repair as candidate controller). Four trajectories (§4.5).

**Illustrations (§1, §3.9, §5, pp. 2, 8, 11–15).** The seven opening examples, Table 1, *sheeps*, *pobedit'*, LBE, garden paths. No new data; literature examples and author judgments.

**Simulation existence check (§4.2–4.3, §8; pp. 9–10, 22).** Separated regimes arise under some adoption/retention settings; evidence updating alone can't produce them. Entirely in the supplement.

**Empirical results.** None original in the main text, except an obliquely reported dative rescoring on Spoken BNC2014 (§6.1, §6.5, pp. 17, 19–20) and an "agreement corpus probe" (§3.8, p. 8) referred to but not described. Cited external results (Christensen & Nyvad, Franjieh et al., Verkerk et al.) are explicitly said not to validate OVMG.

**Predictions (§6.1, pp. 15–18).** Seven conditional predictions with stated failure conditions.

## Gate 2: judgments or restrictions insufficiently grounded

**MATERIAL: none.** All judgment-dependent cases are presented as illustrations or as predictions conditional on independent evidence, not as results.

**MINOR:**
- p. 13, §5.5, ex. (5) and "*the black sheeps of the family*, where the irregular plural might signal a figurative usage": garbled. *Sheeps* is the regular plural; the sentence says the irregular one signals figurative use. As written the illustration contradicts itself.
- p. 8, Table 1 and p. 13, §5.5, "*I have 25 years* … concentrated non-licensing … an established competitor is encountered constantly": the paper's own rule (p. 11, §4.5, "requires independent evidence that the niche was rich, the target was a live candidate, and a competitor repeatedly won") is applied to LBE ("putative", "candidate") but not to the title example, which gets an unhedged concentrated-preemption diagnosis on no stated evidence.
- p. 9, §4.2, "*going to* futurate", "retreat of the French passé simple"; p. 11, §5.2, "Turkish evidential marking" obligatory: uncited historical/typological claims.
- p. 7, §3.6, "Centre embedding can feel very bad even when the speaker accepts the construction once its parse is made clear": an empirical claim about speaker behaviour, uncited.
- p. 14, §5.5, "the niche is common" for *pobedit'* 1sg and "speakers report not knowing how to say it": the opportunity claim is asserted; Sims (2023) is cited for the mixture of sources, not for niche density.
- p. 7, §3.6, the four contrasting phenomenologies ("that's impossible" / "I don't know how to say it") are asserted as what the model predicts; no source for the reports themselves.

## Gate 3: links a reader can't evaluate without the supplement

**MATERIAL:**
1. **p. 1 abstract; p. 9–10, §4.2–4.3; p. 22, §8. "Simulations show that specified adoption conditions can generate sharply separated population patterns; greater precision in an analyst's evidence-based estimate can't" / "Evidence updating alone is insufficient."** The main text gives no model structure (what "adoption response, opportunity, memory, and competition" are as variables), no parameter ranges, no figure, no summary statistic, and no statement of why updating alone can't bimodalize (which could be true by construction, or a substantive result; the reader can't tell which). This is the paper's one non-verbal contribution and it's presented as established in abstract and conclusion. Status of the claim conflates "we built a model in which X happens" with "X is a result".
2. **p. 3, §1, "machine-checked structural results"; p. 22, "machine-checked structural correspondence."** Nothing says what proposition was checked. A reader can't distinguish a checked theorem about the dynamics from a checked type-correspondence of definitions.
3. **p. 17, §6.1 ("a corpus production model is well calibrated on the recipient choice within its high-frequency core, the graded, single-peaked signature OVMG predicts") and pp. 19–20, §6.5 ("a frozen version of such a model can be rescored on later British conversation in the Spoken BNC2014 … The released rows fix realization … length, animacy, definiteness, and pronominality carry across varieties, while verb base rates travel worse, and even the transportable relations become overconfident out of domain").** These are specific empirical findings (calibration, transportability, overconfidence) stated in the present tense with no numbers, method, or location. The reader can't tell whether this is the author's unreported analysis, a gloss of Bresnan et al. (2007), or something in Supplementary File 3. It is offered as "the worked case" for the non-bimodal control prediction, so it carries evidential weight it hasn't been given.

**MINOR:**
- p. 8, §3.8, "The agreement corpus probe reported there": an empirical result (agreement of what, in COCA) is invoked as an illustration of discipline but never described; the data statement (p. 22) confirms it exists.
- p. 19, §6.5, "The formal supplement states the factorization and the restricted conditions under which an attributable omission provides approximately direct evidence of non-licensing": this is the claimed advance over Stefanowitsch; the verbal version in §4.1 is enough to follow, not to evaluate.
- p. 17, §6.1, history-dependent loss/recovery boundaries: properly flagged as conditional, but the conditions are only in the supplement.

## Gate 4: falsifiers with unclear target

**MATERIAL: none.** Most predictions name a target and a failure condition (operator hypothesis p. 4–5; satiation rank order p. 16; L2 transfer p. 16; repair type p. 17; re-licensing p. 17).

**MINOR:**
- p. 16, §6.1, "Concentration loss before categorical decline": a supporting result is stated, but no failure condition. Dispersion-leading outcomes are pre-absorbed as "conditional" on cohort replacement, network loss, heterogeneous retention, or "a specified judgment mapping", so it's unclear what observation would count against the destabilization claim.
- p. 16–17, §6.1, "Soft alternations as a non-bimodal control": if the dative turned out bimodal, the text ("separated licensing only where … independently motivated adoption costs support it") lets adoption costs be adjusted; whether this tests the dynamics or the cost assumptions isn't stated.
- p. 10, §4.5, obligatorification: "a contrast that became obligatory without repeated losses in a well-attested niche … would weaken the proposal". Target is clear (the historical claim), but it's unclear how "repeated losses" would be observed in historical data, so the falsifier isn't operational.
- p. 21, §7.1 and p. 6, §3.5: failures are routed to the decision layer or to "the projection" ("narrowed, split, or abandoned"), never to the four-condition architecture. The paper is candid about this, but a reader can't identify any result that would count against the profile itself rather than a module.

## Summary

The paper communicates its evidential structure well at the level of labels (it repeatedly says what is a proposal, an illustration, an existence check, or a conditional prediction). The gate failures are concentrated in Gate 3: the one quantitative result (separated regimes; updating alone can't) and one empirical result (dative transportability) are asserted in the main text with nothing a reader can check, and the latter isn't even located.

GATE: FAIL
