# Independent theoretical adjudication: selective grammaticality

You are reviewing the proposed reconstruction of a theoretical linguistics
paper. Work only from this textual brief. Do not search, use tools, inspect local

## The explanandum

Why do speakers classify some readily interpretable deviations as errors of
grammar, while classifying others as errors or differences of lexical choice,
style, pronunciation, pragmatics, orthography, or social conduct?

The paper is theoretical. Empirical predictions matter because they discipline
the proposal, but repair or experimental design must not replace this question.

## Background commitments

1. Grammaticality, acceptability, processing difficulty, interpretability,
   prescriptive sanction, and frequency are not coextensive.
2. The target of a response (what the speaker takes to be wrong) must be
   separated from the cause of that response (processing, exposure, schooling,
   stigma, task demands, and so on).
3. The paper adopts Lars Heltoft and Peter Nørgård-Sørensen's paradigm
   architecture only to the extent compatible with a projectibility-first view
   of categories. A strict paradigm has a bounded set of alternatives recurring
   in a syntagmatic domain and organized by a language-specific semantic frame.
4. Public-update function is not proposed as a complete definition of grammar.
   It is one central property among a cluster of properties. Clause type, tense,
   evidentiality, polarity, person, switch reference, and related systems help
   configure what public update an utterance proposes and how it is to be taken.
5. Information-theoretically, a marker can be locally redundant and its value
   readily recoverable yet still select among a small conventional repertoire of
   consequential update instructions. Surprisal does not by itself identify
   grammatical membership or fault locus.
6. Repair is a possible held-out consequence, not an ontology meter and not the
   explanandum.

## Projectibility-first constraint

The category must earn its keep through non-trivial projections. A projective
claim must state what observed source feature warrants expecting which target
outcome, for what bearer, population, conditions, scope, timescale, and
tolerance. Membership diagnostics cannot double as the only predicted outcomes.
Warrant must be kept separate from world-side claims. Stability and causal order
are initially incomparable; maintenance and corrective control are stronger
claims requiring independent evidence. Do not recommend homeostatic-property-
cluster language merely because the properties recur.

The expected category may be graded internally and have an edge that is real but
epistemically difficult to locate. Its boundary can move over time and across
situations. That is not permission to rescue every failed projection by
post-hoc rescoping.

## Candidate layered answer

- Grammatical fault attribution targets violations of community-specific,
  constrained form--value choice systems.
- Paradigm structure is one main way those systems become identifiable and
  non-negotiable.
- Constructional licensing may be another route.
- Public-update function is one central cluster property that may explain why a
  core subset of grammatical systems is especially entrenched, consequential,
  and projectible.
- Open lexical, stylistic, and social alternatives can be conventional,
  sanctioned, and predictable without thereby constituting grammatical choice
  systems.

## Cases the answer must handle

1. **Wrong operator value:** a switch-reference form selects the wrong reference
   relation. The intended update changes even if a hearer can infer what was
   meant.
2. **Value-preserving exponent error:** English *goed* expresses PAST but uses an
   excluded exponent. It can be transparently interpretable and still be called
   ungrammatical.
3. **Complementation/collocation boundary:** English *depend of* is often called
   ungrammatical. One possibility is that complementation normally selects among
   a small set of grammatical functions, those functions select among a small set
   of phrasal categories, and selection of a PP introduces a lexically restricted
   choice among particular prepositions. On that analysis, *depend of* lies at a
   grammar--lexicon boundary: constructional licensing plus collocation, not a
   counterexample that must be assigned wholly to one side.
4. **Lexical/style/social alternatives:** a marked collocation, an accent feature,
   a stylistic alternative, or a socially sanctioned expression may attract
   correction without normally being treated as a grammatical instruction.
