## Convergence

Both reviewers returned **PROCEED**, conditional on structural revision. They
agreed on five points.

1. The proposal is circular unless the relevant system profile is identified
   before grammaticality labels, corrections, or repair outcomes are observed.
2. The bearer should be a particular choice system in a community, not an error
   token, a whole language, or an abstract operator label.
3. Heltoft and Nørgård-Sørensen supply an important paradigm architecture, but a
   strict paradigm should be treated as a strong or limiting case rather than a
   necessary-and-sufficient definition of grammar.
4. Public-update function should explain projections associated with a core
   subset—especially entrenchment, non-negotiability, or the consequences of
   wrong-value errors—rather than fix grammatical membership.
5. The *depend of* analysis is useful only if its complementation and lexical-
   selection layers are assigned structurally before fault-attribution outcomes
   are examined.

Both also identified the same principal threat: distributional notions such as
closure, obligatoriness, and uniformity can covertly inherit classifications from
the grammatical tradition. The manuscript must therefore state which evidence
places a system in profile space and show that this evidence does not include the
outcome to be predicted.

## Ox Alpha's distinctive contributions

- It proposed two separate jobs for properties: **membership/identification**
  properties (bounded repertoire, recurrence, semantic frame, community
  specificity) and **projection-grounding** properties (public-update
  consequence, contrastive consequence under recoverability, acquisition
  entrenchment, and—only with independent evidence—corrective control).
- It treated *depend of* as a licensing chain and proposed a pre-outcome rule:
  an item is grammatically selected only when it realizes a function, category,
  or item choice governed by the construction's licensing structure, not merely
  when it co-occurs with the construction.
- It warned that school rules can mimic closed choice systems and that lexical
  innovations such as *irregardless* make “closed versus open” too weak a
  demarcation on its own.
- It proposed held-out projections for grammatical fault attribution and for the
  persistence of fault attribution under complete interpretive recovery.

## Fable's distinctive contributions

- It supplied the strongest overgeneration test: phonology and orthography are
  also conventional, constrained systems. Closure alone therefore cannot explain
  why their deviations receive different fault-domain labels.
- It supplied a useful undergeneration and change case: as *who/whom* loses
  production uniformity, attribution may migrate from grammar toward style or
  pedantry. Population concentration should therefore be modeled as a graded
  profile property rather than assumed.
- It distinguished spontaneous system-tracking from institutionally transmitted
  grammar labels. Split-infinitive-style shibboleths predict a dissociation
  between unprimed detection and school-framed metalinguistic classification.
- It proposed an ordinal prediction for the complementation chain: function
  selection, category selection, lexically restricted preposition selection,
  and open collocation should show progressively less grammar-directed and more
  variable attribution.
- It argued that update-neutral grammar, including declension-class and concord
  errors, is a necessary matched comparison for the public-update hypothesis.

## Adjudication

The convergence is strong, but neither proposed thesis should be adopted
wholesale.

- Both reviewers turned several graded properties into de facto membership
  conditions. That conflicts with a projectibility-first account and with the
  OVMG separation of coverage, compatibility, saturation, licensing, and
  read-out. Boundedness, obligatoriness, and population concentration should be
  coordinates of a profile, not a conjunctive gate.
- “Community uniformity” is especially unsuitable as a constitutive condition.
  Variation and change are part of the explanandum. Concentration across relevant
  speakers and situations can instead affect how far and how confidently a
  projection travels.
- Public update should not yet be said to explain historical existence,
  enforcement, or resistance to loss. Those are possible projective targets.
  The present evidence does not establish the causal order, let alone maintenance
  or corrective control.
- Phonology and orthography should not be excluded by stipulation. They are
  neighboring constrained systems whose distinct structural level and practice
  supply a genuine test of whether the proposed profile predicts fault-domain
  attribution.

## Decision carried into the reconstruction plan

The paper will use **grammatical choice system** for the bearer and
**grammatical-choice profile** for the independently assessed, graded structure
from which projections are made. Strict paradigms occupy a strong region of that
profile space. Constructional licensing supplies a second and potentially
layered route. Public-update contribution is one coordinate of the profile and
grounds a narrower family of operator-specific projections; it neither defines
grammar nor automatically establishes a causal or maintenance claim.

Repair remains one possible target outcome. The governing projection concerns
selective grammatical fault attribution itself.
