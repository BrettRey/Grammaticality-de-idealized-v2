# Clean-Room Review: "Why clause structure is judged like tense and agreement"

## 1. Central claim, in my words, at its strongest

Speakers police some contrasts as "grammar" (agreement, tense, clause type) and others as style, etiquette, or mere content choice. The manuscript explains this selectivity with a two-stage account: (a) **recruitment** — contrasts that conventionally supply *instructions for public update* (what move an utterance counts as, who is committed to what, what next moves are licensed) enter a privileged "operator stratum"; (b) **maintenance** — once such contrasts occupy closed paradigms encountered in large opportunity sets, policing detaches from token-level update value and attaches to *paradigm integrity itself*. The strongest version is genuinely strong: it predicts that grammaticality talk should cross-cut substantive modality (tone, gesture, prosody can host operators), that locally redundant errors (*goed*, gender concord) should be policed categorically, and that repair, satiation, and processing signatures should cluster on operator contrasts rather than on morphosyntax as a substance.

## 2. Argument reconstructed

- **P1.** Grammaticality policing is selective across contrast types. (§1)
- **P2.** Some form–value relations conventionally supply public-update instructions. (Public-update condition, §3.1)
- **P3.** Some relations belong to restricted, closed, recurrently selected repertoires. (Repertoire condition, §3.1)
- **C1.** Relations satisfying P2+P3 are operators; they constitute the operator stratum.
- **P4.** P2 is a recruitment condition; P3 plus opportunity sets is a maintenance condition; policing attaches to paradigm integrity under maintenance. (§3.1)
- **C2.** Value-intact exponent errors and attenuated-value concord errors are policed because of P4, not failed updates. (§3.2, §6)
- **C3.** Clausal architecture patterns with tense/agreement because it packages operator values (clause type, argument linking, scope, reference tracking). (§4)
- **C4.** Predictions: form-targeted repair profiles, satiation resistance, ERP/reanalysis signatures, cross-linguistic clustering including tonal/gestural exponents. (§3.3, §8)

**Hidden premises worth surfacing:**
- **HP1.** "The asterisk" names a single phenomenon with a single target. The paper partially concedes heterogeneity (detector vs. target, ideological policing, §7), but the unifying move is doing more work than acknowledged.
- **HP2.** "Update instruction" is a well-typed category separable from content contribution. The paper's own concession that *congratulate* and *accuse* "massively reshape continuations" (§7.2, "The issue isn't that operators always reduce more entropy") shows this needs the operational test, which (see §4 below) doesn't fully deliver.
- **HP3.** Preemption/entrenchment bridges "form not produced" to "form judged illicit." This bridge is borrowed (Bybee, O'Connor) and asserted rather than argued; non-production and normative policing are different facts.

## 3. Category-membership tests (six cases)

**(a) Subject–verb agreement, English — central case.** Satisfies both conditions; predicted form-targeted repair. But third-person *-s* is dropped pervasively in casual speech with no repair at all. The paper pre-empts this ("Overt repair is rare overall, but when it does occur…", §8, Prediction 1) — a conditionalization that threatens to make the prediction unfalsifiable: non-repair is always explainable as rarity of repair. **PLAUSIBLE CONCERN.**

**(b) Japanese *ka* — central case that comes apart.** Clause type is the paradigm operator, yet the paper concedes that omitted *ka* "may yield a declarative construal, a colloquial intonation-marked question, or some other licensed move" (§4). So a core operator member predicts *non*-categorical policing, and the rescue is an extra variable — obligatoriness — not present in the two membership conditions. If operator membership doesn't predict policing without a further stipulation, the membership category's diagnostic value is diminished. The paper is honest about this ("operator membership without automatic failure") but does not integrate the lesson into the conditions themselves. **VERIFIED DEFECT** (criteria/prediction mismatch, acknowledged but unresolved).

**(c) French \*le hiver — boundary case that strains the theory.** The paper classifies it at the "expression-shape/operator boundary" because the determiner repertoire "also participates in reference setting" (§3.2). But elision before *h-* words is largely lexically listed (*le héros*, *le hibou* vs. *l'homme*), arbitrary, and — by the paper's own analysis — makes no difference to update type: *le* vs. *l'* does not change what move is made or what responses are licensed. On the paper's own three-strata table, this should be an expression-shape mismatch ("not a word" profile), yet it attracts grammaticality talk. Either the public-update condition is not necessary even for recruitment, or the classification is ad hoc. As analyzed, this is evidence for a simpler "closed paradigm closure + form-selection suffices" rival (see §6). **VERIFIED DEFECT.**

**(d) \*depend of — boundary case, under-argued.** Grouped with \*goed as "a closed form-selection dependency" (§3.2). But preposition selection looks like lexical choice, which the paper elsewhere assigns to the payload stratum. The paper's line between policed "closed form-selection dependencies" and unpoliced "idioms and fixed collocations… near misses" (§3.1) is that the former "serve as combinatorial values in a contrastive system" — but *of/on* after *depend* is exactly a mutually exclusive choice in a describable environment. Why is this grammatical while idiom-internal word choice is not? No criterion is given that separates them. **VERIFIED DEFECT.**

**(e) Interjections — boundary case, circularity risk.** Membership turns on whether the interjection is "conventionalized into a closed, distributionally accountable paradigm" (§3.2) — but that is just restating the repertoire condition. The §3.3 diagnostics are presented as identifying the stratum "independently" of judgments ("Closure, scope, and opportunity structure identify operator contrasts independently"), yet closure *is* condition (ii) and opportunity structure is the maintenance variable. Diagnostics 1 and 3 partially restate the membership conditions rather than independently operationalizing them. **VERIFIED DEFECT** (partial circularity between §3.1 and §3.3).

**(f) T/V pronouns — boundary case with near-zero discriminative power.** The prediction of "overlap" (§5.2) — "a wrong form may be socially sanctionable… and structurally repairable" — is compatible with virtually any observed outcome, since both profiles are predicted to co-occur. **PLAUSIBLE CONCERN.**

**(g) Gender concord — the best case in the paper.** The operational prediction (manipulate gender on inanimates, hold payload constant, measure next-turn shift; §6) is the manuscript's most falsifiable and most valuable commitment. But the recruitment story itself — "Gender and noun-class systems plausibly enter the operator region through reference tracking" (§6) — is asserted with no mechanism, no citation, and no diachronic evidence. **PLAUSIBLE CONCERN** (evidential bridge missing).

## 4. Non-triviality of the predictions

**The operational test for condition (i) does not discriminate.** "A contrast supplies an update instruction iff manipulating it, while holding payload constant, shifts the distribution over next-turn types" (§3.1). Slurs shift next-turn distributions massively (addressees exit, sanction); assessments — a payload-class item by the paper's lights — license second assessments as an adjacency pair; accent shifts can change response type. The test as stated classifies socially charged payload and indexical items as operators. The intended restriction (answers/acknowledgements/refusals as *update-type* responses vs. sanction as *person-targeted*) is a coder-level distinction the paper gestures at but does not specify. **VERIFIED DEFECT.**

**Prediction novelty.** Predictions (3) and (5) largely recapitulate the known N400/P600 morphosyntax–semantics dissociation; the paper concedes this ("This isn't a novelty claim," §8) and that the Akan tonal result already exists (Tsiwah et al.). The genuinely new, discriminating predictions are the corpus repair-profile study and the satiation asymmetry. The satiation prediction, however, uses left-branch extraction as its example — a construction whose operator status is established only by leaning on the companion paper's preemption story, so the classification and the prediction are not independent. **PLAUSIBLE CONCERN.**

**Free parameters absorb counterexamples.** Opportunity-set size ("categoricality… weaker and more register-bound," §3.3), activation profile, and accountability profile (§7) can each be tuned post hoc to accommodate any non-categorical operator or any policed non-operator. The disconfirmation condition in §8 is commendably explicit, but it controls only surprisal, frequency, prosody, and task — not recoverability of the intended form or epistemic access, which CA work (Schegloff & Jefferson, cited) shows drive other-correction format independently of error class. **PLAUSIBLE CONCERN.**

**Missing comparators.** (i) Written-register policing: comma splices, *less/fewer*, spelling — policed as "grammar," closed paradigms, no plausible update-instruction role. Not addressed. (ii) The motivating data for categorical policing (*goed*, agreement) are disproportionately from instructed/L2/child contexts; the accountability-profile machinery concedes this calibrates policing, which should prompt a comparison with policing in non-schooled communities. (iii) Discourse markers: closed-ish sets configuring uptake that the theory predicts should *not* be policed — but no criterion explains why they fail the repertoire condition while *depend on* passes it. **PLAUSIBLE CONCERN.**

## 5. Explanatory levels and causal vocabulary

The paper is unusually careful at two joints: target vs. detector ("Felt ungrammaticality is detector output… they aren't the target itself," §7) and recruitment vs. maintenance ("The two conditions do different causal work," §3.1). Three transitions nonetheless lack bridges:

1. **Recruitment mechanism.** "Contrasts are recruited because they serve as shared infrastructure" (Abstract) — the diachronic path is never specified beyond a footnote conceding the conditions are "mutually constitutive" (§3.1, fn. 4). Why gender, specifically, via reference tracking? No mechanism. **PLAUSIBLE CONCERN.**
2. **From non-production to policing.** Preemption explains why a form is not produced; the step to its being *judged illicit* and *correctable* is carried by O'Connor 2019 and Millar 2004 at the level of assertion, not argument. **PLAUSIBLE CONCERN.**
3. **Level of the maintenance mechanism.** The paper mixes individual-level processes (entrenchment, satiation, prediction) with population-level ones (community repertoire, norm centres, sanction) without saying which does which work, or how they relate. "Policing attaches" — who polices, at what timescale? §7 supplies social architecture but not the linkage to the psycholinguistic predictions of §8. **PLAUSIBLE CONCERN.**

## 6. Strongest rival and discriminating observations

**Rival: paradigm-closure-plus-frequency.** Grammaticality talk targets whatever occupies closed paradigms with high token frequency and strong competitor frequency — no public-update condition needed. This handles \*goed, gender, tense, agreement, *l'hiver*, and *depend on* at least as well, predicts the same repair formats (high-frequency alternation sets invite "you mean X"), the same satiation profile (entrenched violations resist satiation), and the same ERP signatures (known dissociation). A second rival — school/prescriptivist ideology codifies morphology — handles negative concord and the L2 data directly.

**What discriminates:** the manuscript's best weapon is the cross-substance prediction (tonal/gestural operators pattern as grammar; segmentally identical sociophonetic tone variation does not), which defeats both the "morphosyntax substance" and "school ideology" rivals. Against *closure-plus-frequency*, the discriminating observations are: (i) matched closed paradigms, matched for frequency and opportunity, differing in update-instruction role — does policing differ? (the gender test is an instance; a discourse-marker/collocation comparison is another); (ii) repair-profile corpora from communities without formal schooling; (iii) the \*le hiver problem run in reverse — if arbitrary allomorphy with no update-role difference is policed as grammar, the rival wins and condition (i) must be demoted to diachrony. The paper currently treats (iii) as a friendly boundary case; it should be treated as a test.

## 7. Audience-friction check

- **Title.** Presupposes the explanandum and leads with jargon ("public-update operators") unfamiliar to sociolinguists and typologists; acceptable but friction-generating. **PRESENTATION ISSUE.**
- **Abstract.** "Value-intact exponent errors" is undefined at abstract level; "protocol headers" is glib for a general linguistics audience; the abstract asserts cross-linguistic clustering as a prediction without noting it is largely untested. Also, "categorical judgments… should cluster around recruited operator paradigms" overclaims relative to the paper's own three-strata theory: expression-shape mismatches ("not a possible word") are equally categorical, so categoricality does not cluster on operators — only repair profile does. **VERIFIED DEFECT** (abstract vs. theory).
- **Opening sections.** Honest about the "bad grammar" footnote problem; the *valeur* footnote is helpful; the three strata arrive quickly and the expression-shape stratum does heavy lifting while underdeveloped. **PRESENTATION ISSUE.**
- **Conclusion.** "Not corrective-control mechanisms in the stronger homeostatic sense" (§9) denies an accusation no standalone reader has made — internal-debate residue from companion papers; new citations appear in the conclusion. **PRESENTATION ISSUE.**
- **Source hygiene.** The source contains a commented-out companion-paper section ("Integration with the MVMG architecture"), a "REVISION REPORT FOR BRETT" block, multiple BRETT-VERIFY notes, and a "Disagreement memo." These must not survive to submission. Heavy dependence on six 2026 companion/unverifiable citations (reynolds2026*, gibson2026, weinberger2026) means key classifications (LBE as operator niche; interjection data; stability claims) cannot be checked from this manuscript. **PRESENTATION ISSUE / external claims flagged for verification** (Amele forms, Ngwi TAMP claims, Mahowald et al. 30-language result, Tsiwah et al. ERP result — all need verification against sources not supplied).

## Ranked revision list

1. **Repair the operational test for condition (i).** Specify the update-type taxonomy and coding procedure so that next-turn-shift analysis excludes sanction-, stance-, and assessment-driven response shifts; as written, the "iff" test classifies slurs and evaluative payload as operators. (§3.1, "shifts the distribution over next-turn types")
2. **Demote or defend the synchronic role of the public-update condition.** Table 2's own cell ("historically/recruited") concedes condition (i) fails for a central error class; either state plainly that present policing is predicted by closure + opportunity alone (and adjust title/abstract accordingly), or give condition (i) synchronic work to do. (§3.2, Table 2; Abstract)
3. **Draw the policed/unpoliced closed-selection boundary.** Provide criteria distinguishing \*depend of and \*le hiver from idioms, discourse markers, *less/fewer*, and arbitrary allomorphy — or concede that paradigm closure without update role also attracts grammaticality talk, and integrate that into the theory rather than the "boundary" label. (§3.2, "closed form-selection dependency"; §3.1, "near misses")
4. **Add controls to the repair-profile and satiation predictions** — matched recoverability of the target form, epistemic access, error class per Schegloff–Jefferson — and address the instructional/L2 confound in the motivating data, ideally with a non-schooled-community comparison. (§8, Prediction 1; §7, "accountability profile")
5. **Source and framing hygiene.** Remove all internal comment blocks and companion-paper dependencies (or summarize their needed content inline); verify external claims (Amele, Ngwi, Mahowald et al., Tsiwah et al.); fix the abstract's categoricality-clustering overclaim, which contradicts the paper's own expression-shape stratum.

## Verdict

**REPAIRABLE WITHOUT NEW THEORY.** The recruitment/maintenance split is already the paper's real theory and is coherent, self-aware about the detector/target distinction, and generative of at least two genuinely discriminating empirical programs (the gender next-turn test; cross-substance tonal/gestural comparisons). The defects are of operationalization, boundary-drawing, and framing — not of architecture. The one change with theoretical consequence is item 2: if the public-update condition is honestly demoted to diachrony, the paper's synchronic engine is "closed paradigm + opportunity set," and it should say so rather than letting the update story continue to do rhetorical work it no longer does.
