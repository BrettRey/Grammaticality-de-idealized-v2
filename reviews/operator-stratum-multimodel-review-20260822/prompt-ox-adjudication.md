# Refutation-first adjudication: operator-stratum paper

You are the final external adjudicator for a revision decision, not another reviewer adding a vote. Read the target manuscript and all three raw reviews in this bundle. Re-open the reviewers' strongest claims, reject any that do not survive contact with the text, and identify the smallest coherent theory that remains.

The target is a conceptual linguistics paper for a broad expert audience. Its companion OVMG paper was just repaired after formal, simulation, and Lean audits. Import only lessons that genuinely bear on this target: distinguish population/conventional facts from evidence and subjective read-outs; distinguish stabilization, maintenance, and corrective control; do not let a diagnostic define the world-side category it estimates; do not let historical recruitment silently replace synchronic membership; and require projectible claims to specify what is projected, by what bearer, over what scope, with what tolerance, on what warrant.

## Evidence that must be adjudicated rather than ignored

### French *le hiver*

The clean-room reviews object that *le hiver* has no token-level public-update difference and looks like arbitrary, lexically conditioned allomorphy. The companion OVMG paper supplies a general analysis that the target does not currently reproduce. Its Turkish appendix distinguishes an operator value from its exponent: vowel backness is not a public-update value, but plural and past are; a disharmonic suffix is an identifiable yet unlicensed exponent choice in a dense operator-exponent niche. It concludes that such errors "don't mis-set a public-update contrast themselves, but they choose an unlicensed exponent for a closed inflectional operator in a dense niche." The proposed analogy is: French definite reference is the operator value; *le/l'* are contextually licensed exponents in a closed determiner paradigm; *le hiver* preserves definite reference but selects an unlicensed exponent. Decide whether this makes *le hiver* a principled operator-exponent case, merely a suggestive analogy needing empirical/citation support, or still an ad hoc rescue. Separate the adequacy of the analysis from the target paper's present failure to state it.

### *depend of*

No companion-paper passage or other project source found so far gives *depend of* the same operator-exponent analysis. The target calls it a "closed form-selection dependency" and says the relevant lexical relation is recoverable. Decide whether governed preposition selection actually realizes an operator value, whether it belongs only to the broader OVMG licensing route, or whether it can remain as an explicit non-operator comparison. Do not retain it merely because speakers give categorical judgments.

### Public update

For the intended reader, begin with this reader-first gloss: some grammatical contrasts tell interlocutors how to treat an utterance—as a question rather than an assertion, as committing one participant rather than another, or as linking a referent or role across clauses. The project calls this a public-update role. Determine whether the technical notion can be made both intelligible and discriminating. In particular, adjudicate token-level versus paradigm-level update contribution, diagnostic proxies such as next-turn distributions versus comprehension probes, and diachronic recruitment versus synchronic membership.

### Membership, opportunity, and gender

The target says low-opportunity contrasts may still be operators while categoricality weakens. The companion currently makes high opportunity a membership condition. The target's gender argument relies on locally redundant tokens inside paradigms that sometimes carry reference-tracking or role-allocation information. Decide whether membership should be (a) closed paradigm plus paradigm-level expected public-update contribution, with opportunity density modulating concentration/categoricality, or (b) something else. Preserve a genuine falsifier; do not immunize every categorical contrast by inventing a historical recruitment story.
