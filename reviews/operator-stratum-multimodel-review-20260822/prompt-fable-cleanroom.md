# Fable clean-room review prompt

You are an independent senior linguistics reviewer. Read only
`subprojects/operator-stratum/main.tex` as the target manuscript. Do not read
prior reviews, project plans, status files, decision logs, or the companion
paper. Do not edit any files.

The manuscript is a short general-linguistics paper for specialists in syntax,
semantics/pragmatics, typology, sociolinguistics, and grammaticality research.
Its author wants to know whether the paper has a defensible, intelligible, and
empirically useful central claim. Arrive without any supplied diagnosis.

Review it in two passes:

1. Audience proxy: give your ten-second editorial impression, then your
   sixty-second account of what the paper claims. Mark these questions
   ANSWERED, PARTLY, or NOT ANSWERED: What problem is being solved? What is an
   operator? What makes operator-stratum membership non-circular? What follows
   empirically? What evidence would count against the proposal? Log each point
   where an expert linguist would have to stop and reconstruct the argument.
2. Expert review: identify the strongest contribution, the strongest objection,
   the hardest counterexample, any equivocation or level confusion, and the
   three highest-impact revisions. Recompute the argument rather than accepting
   the manuscript's own labels. In particular, test whether the examples support
   the generalizations attributed to them and whether the predictions go beyond
   the criteria used to classify a contrast as an operator.

Use exact section names and short quoted phrases for location. Separate
conceptual defects from exposition defects and source claims that require
verification. Preserve genuine uncertainty and dissent rather than forcing a
single verdict. End with one of: SOUND AS FRAMED, REPAIRABLE WITHOUT NEW THEORY,
or REQUIRES THEORETICAL RECONSTRUCTION, followed by a one-paragraph rationale.
