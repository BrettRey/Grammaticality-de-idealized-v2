# Targeted follow-up: are these level or category errors?

Answer after reading the preceding Ox Alpha adjudication in this bundle.

The author proposes this analysis:

> Complementation in general is a matter of community conventions. It is never fully dealt with lexically: typically the complement is represented by a category and/or grammatical function, while particular heads, especially with highly grammaticalized prepositions, narrow the complement to a PP headed by one or a very small set of prepositions.

We are considering the following two-axis treatment:

- **Constructional licensing:** a community-level fact about which complement construction is licensed, potentially represented schematically and inherited rather than stored as an atomic lexical collocation.
- **Operator role:** a functional fact about whether a grammatical contrast configures public update (clause type, commitment, scope, participant or reference linking). Some complement constructions intersect this axis; others do not.

On this proposal, *depend on/upon* is a schematic but lexically narrowed complement construction. *Depend of* can be categorically unlicensed through dense competitor preemption without *on* being a public-update operator. Complementizers, case/adposition paradigms, or other complement exponents count as operators only when they realize an update-configuring grammatical value. Operator-exponent licensing is a third, dependent relation: the value can be correct while its exponent is unlicensed, as with *goed*, Turkish suffix disharmony, and perhaps French *le hiver*.

Adjudicate directly:

1. Were the manuscript or the reviews committing a **level error** by moving among community-level licensing, speaker representation/evidence, token interpretation, and correction behaviour?
2. Were they committing a **category error** by treating constructional licensing, lexical conditioning, operator-value selection, and operator-exponent selection as mutually exclusive classifications—or by collapsing them into one class?
3. Is the proposed two-axis treatment coherent? If not, state the minimal replacement.
4. Classify *depend on/upon*, *le/l' hiver*, *goed/went*, Turkish harmonic suffix choice, complementizer selection, and case/adposition role marking on the relevant axes.
5. Give one paragraph suitable for the target paper that explains the distinction to a typical linguist without importing the OVMG formalism.

Do not answer merely that the issue is "nuanced." Say exactly which inferences are invalid, at what level, and what the corrected ontology is.
