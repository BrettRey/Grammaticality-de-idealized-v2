# Review: *Why clause structure is judged like tense and agreement: public-update operators and grammaticality*

## Pass 1: Audience proxy

### Ten-second impression

A functional-interactional account of where the asterisk lands, built from three imported vocabularies at once: common-ground/commitment semantics (Stalnaker, Farkas & Bruce, Murray), usage-based entrenchment (Bybee, preemption, "opportunity sets"), and a networking metaphor (headers, payload, codebook) dressed as information theory. The abstract is honest in a way that's also a warning: by its second paragraph it concedes that the headline mechanism (public update) is *not* what polices the familiar errors (*goed*, gender), and hands that job to "paradigm integrity." An editor's first question is whether the public-update half survives that concession or becomes decoration on an entrenchment account.

### Sixty-second account of the claim

Grammaticality talk targets a "stratum" of form–value contrasts the paper calls operators. A contrast is an operator when (i) it supplies an instruction for how the utterance updates shared commitments (what move it is, who's committed, what replies are licensed) and (ii) it belongs to a closed, recurrently selected, mutually exclusive paradigm. Condition (i) explains why a contrast gets *recruited* into the policed region; condition (ii), plus large opportunity sets and preemption, explains why policing then attaches to the paradigm rather than to the token's communicative usefulness. Clause structure is "grammar" because it packages such operators (clause type, argument linking, dependency scope, reference tracking); accent and open-class lexis are not, because they're indexical or open. Operators are header-like: they set the decoding frame for material in scope. Five predictions follow (repair, satiation, tonal exponents, sociolinguistic stratification, ERP globality).

### The five questions

**What problem is being solved?** ANSWERED. "The puzzle" states it cleanly: "why some form--value relations become non-negotiable infrastructure, policed as membership facts, while others remain negotiable resources." One mismatch: the title's problem (clause structure specifically) is one section of eleven.

**What is an operator?** PARTLY. A definition and two conditions are given in "The operator-stratum hypothesis," but the stated extension outruns them. The paper says "I use the label narrowly," then admits tense, agreement, case, gender concord, number, clause type, polarity, evidentials, egophoricity, switch-reference, complementisers, question particles, T/V pronouns, governed prepositions (*depend on*), determiner allomorphy (*l'hiver*), the obligatory locative of *put*, left-branch extraction, negation, focus and degree particles, tonal TAM, and ASL nonmanual Q-marking. Several of these are not form–value contrasts at all (*depend of*, *le hiver* are form–form selections with no value opposition), and several are not operators in the RRG/FDG sense the paper says it is "continuous with" (argument linking and dependency structure are what RRG operators scope *over*, not operators). The working notion that actually fixes the extension is "a slot inside a closed, high-frequency paradigm," which is broader than the definition and narrower than the label.

**What makes operator-stratum membership non-circular?** PARTLY. The paper sees the risk and addresses it three times ("The starting point is not the judgment itself"; "without making detector output one of the membership conditions"; "Closure, scope, and opportunity structure identify operator contrasts independently"). Condition (i) gets a corpus operationalization (next-turn distribution). Condition (ii) gets none: "mutually exclusive in those environments" and "not freely innovated token by token" are read off the same distributional facts that ground the asterisk, and the paper doesn't say how to read them without judgments. More seriously, for every case that motivates the paper (*goed*, gender, *depend of*, *le hiver*, LBE), membership is established by (ii) plus a historical recruitment story ("plausibly enter the operator region through reference tracking") that is asserted, not checked. So the non-circularity argument works for the easy cases (clause type, polarity), where it's nearly definitional, and is replaced by an unverified etiology for the hard ones.

**What follows empirically?** PARTLY. "Predictions and research strategies" lists five. (1) and (4) restate the explanandum from "Why some contrasts are publicly accountable" (form-targeted vs person-targeted response). (3) is substance-neutrality, which no current theory denies, and the paper concedes the Akan result already exists. (2) and (5) have content: categoricality and satiation-resistance should scale with opportunity-set size, and operator errors should show broader reanalysis than payload errors. Neither is derived from the public-update condition; both follow from closure plus preemption alone.

**What evidence would count against the proposal?** PARTLY. A disconfirmation condition is stated, but it targets the weakest possible contrast (operator errors vs matched payload errors producing *identical* profiles). The gender test in "Gender and noun-class concord as a hard case" is set up so that a null result "falsifies" the synchronic version and is "predicted" by the recruitment/maintenance version; the version the paper defends is thus the one insulated from its own sharpest test. No test of the recruitment claim itself is stated.

### Reader-stop log (where an expert reconstructs)

1. "The puzzle," last paragraph: "closed paradigms with large opportunity sets" arrives before "opportunity set" is defined (first gloss is two sections later, in "Diagnostics").
2. Same paragraph: "*goed* selects the right past-tense value but the wrong exponent" uses *value* as feature value, which the footnote one paragraph earlier says it "isn't to be confused with."
3. "Why some contrasts are publicly accountable," the two misfires: a tense or agreement slip yielding "Wait, who did what?" and "the update couldn't go through." The reader who took §1 seriously stops: the paper has just said these errors are usually value-intact. The motivating example is the strong theory; the defended theory is the weak one.
4. "The operator-stratum hypothesis," condition (i): "manipulating it ... in conversational corpora." One doesn't manipulate in a corpus. Experiment, or natural variation, and the test changes accordingly.
5. Same subsection: "*Yesterday the bill pass* missets the tense value." With *yesterday* present, the past anchor goes through; this is a paradigm-integrity case, not a value error. It demonstrates the maintenance story while being deployed for the update story.
6. Same subsection: "I have five years." What paradigm is this? It reads as a constructional calque, not an operator contrast.
7. "Boundary cases," the table: the "Update instruction?" column changes meaning for one row ("historically/recruited" among yes/no cells). The table is where the paper's two senses of operator collide visibly.
8. "Diagnostics," left-branch extraction: "This paper explains why that interrogative construction is a policed niche." Nothing in the section has explained why *which car* vs *which ... car* is an operator contrast; both are interrogatives with the same update. The reader infers "any deviation inside an operator-packaging construction counts," which is the "all syntax" thesis §4 disclaims.
9. "Diagnostics," last paragraph: three diagnostics (closure, scope, opportunity) are said to identify operators "independently," but the two *conditions* were public update and repertoire. Which diagnostic operationalizes (i)? Not stated.
10. "Why clausal architecture belongs," "projectible rather than merely frequent": unglossed, citing unpublished work; a typologist or sociolinguist has no purchase on the term.
11. Same section: "*I put the book* leaves the clausal instruction incomplete." The reader stops: an obligatory locative complement is now an "operator value." At this point operator means "obligatory element."
12. Same section, Japanese *ka*: the paragraph concludes membership predicts nothing about omission. The example demonstrates lack of bite.
13. "An information-theoretic frame": "Open-class predicates such as *congratulate* and *accuse* can massively reshape continuations too." The reader waits for what distinguishes codebook control from this, and gets "A wrong header can corrupt interpretation of everything in its scope," which is equally true of a wrong matrix verb.
14. "Social technology," negative concord: *I didn't see nobody* is operator value (polarity, closed paradigm) in *both* repertoires. Which repertoire's verdict is the "first-order operator-paradigm mismatch"? The theory can't say.

## Pass 2: Expert review

### Strongest contribution

The recruitment/maintenance split, stated in "The operator-stratum hypothesis" ("The two conditions do different causal work") and cashed out in "Gender and noun-class concord as a hard case." Separating why a contrast *enters* the policed region from why policing *persists* on communicatively idle tokens is the one move that handles *goed*, gender, and *depend of* without pretending they block uptake, and it yields a graded, testable claim: categoricality should scale with opportunity-set size (subjunctive vs agreement; dual vs plural). The target/detector separation in "Social technology" is also worth keeping: judgments as fallible detectors of a population-level membership fact. And the paper is honest at the points where honesty costs it (Pullum: little communicative pressure to comply; overt correction is rare; the Akan ERP result is not new).

### Strongest objection

**The public-update condition is extensionally idle and its explanatory role is untested.** Go through the paper's own exclusions: accent, register, slurs, proper names, interjections, discourse markers, performatives, idioms. Every one of them fails the repertoire condition (ii). The one case the paper says (ii) admits and (i) excludes is phonotactics ("Cross-substance operators": "repertoire-like ... But they don't satisfy the public-update condition"), and phonotactic slots aren't form–value relations, so they fail the precondition before either condition applies. Conversely, nothing is admitted by (i) that (ii) excludes. So (i) classifies nothing. What it does is tell a story about which contrasts *historically* enter closed paradigms. That story is (a) never tested; (b) in tension with the grammaticalization literature the paper cites, where the known selectors are semantic generality and frequency (Bybee), not update relevance; and (c) unnecessary for any of the five predictions, all of which follow from closure, opportunity set, and preemption. The title's answer ("because it packages operators") then reduces to "because clause structure, tense, and agreement are all closed, high-frequency, preempted paradigms," which is an entrenchment account with the update vocabulary on top.

A secondary objection: the foil named in the abstract ("not because syntax is autonomous from meaning") is not a rival on this question. Autonomy of syntax is a claim about how syntactic generalizations are stated, not about why speakers police them, and no autonomy theorist denies tonal or gestural exponents. The real rivals, neither engaged, are (1) pure entrenchment/statistical preemption (the Bybee line the paper borrows from; the *goed*-specific preemption literature is not cited, see source notes) and (2) a deflationary sociolinguistic account on which the metalinguistic category "grammar" is a standardization and schooling artefact. The paper's own concessions feed (2): adult native other-correction of morphosyntax is rare, so the behavioural signature is strongest exactly where competence is asymmetric (children, L2), which is the "accountability profile" hedge.

### Hardest counterexample

**Standard-variety shibboleths inside closed paradigms**: negative concord (the paper's own example), levelled agreement (*we was*), pronoun case (*who* for *whom*, *between you and I*). Each satisfies (i) on the paper's reading (polarity, agreement, argument linking are the paradigm cases) and (ii) (closed, obligatory, enormous opportunity set). The theory therefore predicts categorical, form-targeted, satiation-resistant policing. What's observed: the policing is norm-centre dependent, speaker-targeted, and in the *whom* case has largely lapsed while the paradigm slot persists. The paper's escape in "Social technology" (situation/ascription/identification conditioning) concedes that the stratum fixes the *slot* where grammaticality talk can occur while sociolinguistics fixes the *verdict*. That's a coherent position, but it's much weaker than "wherever categoricality emerges, it should cluster around operator value," because in these cases categoricality emerges on both sides of the same operator contrast. The asterisk is about which *values* are licensed; the theory addresses which *dimensions* attract talk, and "Social technology" says so ("what triggers grammaticality talk, not how such talk is socially deployed"), but the abstract and title don't.

Two further cases worth a sentence each: conjugation/declension class (closed, obligatory, policed, never update-relevant; absorbable only by "the TAM paradigm was recruited, and theme-vowel selection sits inside it," which shows the absorption principle at work) and numeral classifiers (closed, obligatory, form-targeted repair, recruited by semantic classification rather than update). The general point: between paradigm containment, historical recruitment, activation profile, accountability profile, and norm-centre, the account can redescribe any case I tried. That absorptive capacity is itself the finding.

### Equivocation and level confusion

- **"Value"** has four senses in the paper: Saussurean *valeur* (footnote in "The puzzle"); interactional usefulness ("what it rules in or out for uptake"; "update value has attenuated"); feature value ("the right past-tense value but the wrong exponent"); and paradigm member ("one operator value is selected"). The thesis "grammaticality talk tracks a difference in value" sounds explanatory under the first reading and is actually claiming the second, which the hard cases then force the paper to drop.
- **"Update instruction"** slides from the narrow Farkas & Bruce/Murray sense (table management; structuring vs at-issue updates) to a broad "affects how the hearer construes the utterance." Tense, agreement, case, and concord qualify only under the broad sense, and under the broad sense lexical verbs qualify too, as "An information-theoretic frame" concedes.
- **"Operator"** names a role, a paradigm, a slot, and an error type by turns; "operator mismatch" covers wrong value, wrong exponent, and wrong allomorph, which have different predicted profiles (only the first should show the globality signature).
- **Explanatory level** shifts unmarked among community (repertoire, norm-centre), interaction (repair), individual processing (ERP, satiation), and the information-theoretic gloss. "Operators set the codebook" is not located at any level; the globality ERP prediction is derived from the metaphor, not from a processing model. Note also that the "header" image implies precedence, while many operators are final (*ka*) or distributed (concord); the reanalysis prediction needs stating relative to linear position.
- **Comparative concept** (Haspelmath) is analyst-defined for cross-linguistic comparison; using it as the explanans for language-particular judgments is the slippage that notion was introduced to block.
- **Mismatch profiles don't partition the strata** as "Their mismatch profiles differ accordingly" claims: *goed* draws both "not a word" and "you mean *went*"; "you can't say that" is given as the operator profile in "Cross-substance operators" and conceded to be the slur profile too in "Social technology."

### Do the examples support the generalizations?

Mostly they support the maintenance claim and undercut the update claim. The §2 misfires are invented and implausible for agreement. *Bill pass* and *goed* show paradigm policing with the update intact. *I have five years* and *put the book* are constructional/valency cases imported under the label. *Kim wonders that* is complement subcategorization, and the "intended update" gloss concerns the embedded proposition's semantics, not the utterance's move. Amele and Ngwi are presented as "predicted" behaviour, with no data on what speakers do with the wrong marker or tone; Amele appears twice with the same content. *Ka* shows membership without obligatoriness, i.e. no prediction. Negative concord supports the sociolinguistic rival at least as well.

### Three highest-impact revisions

1. **Motivate with the theory you defend.** Rewrite "The puzzle" and "Why some contrasts are publicly accountable" so the opening cases are value-intact exponent errors that are corrected anyway, and state on page one that token-level update value is not the policing condition. The current sequence leads the reader to the synchronic theory and withdraws it in "Gender and noun-class concord."
2. **Make the public-update condition earn its place or demote it.** Either give the recruitment claim a stated diachronic test (which contrasts obligatorify and paradigmaticize, checked against grammaticalization findings, with classifiers, conjugation class, and phonologically assigned gender named and accounted for), or reframe public update as a conjecture about the selector, confined to one section, and let the paper stand as a paradigm-integrity account with a comparative-concept payoff. In either case, operationalize condition (ii) distributionally, as (i) was.
3. **Replace the predictions with discriminating ones.** Against pure entrenchment: state that the synchronic predictions coincide and the only separating test is which contrasts end up closed. Against the ideology account: predict form-targeted repair of operator slots among adults in *unstandardized* communities, where schooling can't supply the category. Split the globality prediction by error type (wrong value vs wrong exponent), and check the opportunity-set claim against the existing satiation record (which violations satiate in Snyder 2000 and Sprouse 2009) rather than citing it.

### Dissent and uncertainty preserved

Whether condition (i) is strictly idle turns on whether phonotactics counts as a form–value relation; a reader who accepts the paper's framing will grant (i) one exclusion. A grammaticalization specialist may read the recruitment/maintenance split as Lehmann's paradigmaticity and obligatoriness plus Bybee under new names; I think the assembly is worth having, but the novelty claim should be modest. A syntactician may not care about the ideology rival; a sociolinguist will press it hard. Keeping "operator" against its RRG sense is a judgment call: memorable, but it invites the exact misreading the paper spends a paragraph disowning.

---

**Verdict: REPAIRABLE WITHOUT NEW THEORY.**

The defensible paper is already inside this one. Its working mechanism is paradigm integrity maintained by closure, large opportunity sets, and preemption, with a three-stratum comparative concept (expression-shape / operator paradigm / payload) that is substance-neutral and usable by typologists; that account handles *goed*, gender, *depend of*, and *l'hiver*, and yields a graded prediction (categoricality scales with opportunity set) worth testing. What doesn't work as stated is the headline: the public-update condition classifies nothing the repertoire condition doesn't, and its real job, explaining which contrasts get recruited, is a diachronic conjecture the paper neither tests nor distinguishes from existing grammaticalization accounts. The repair is subtraction and honest reframing (motivate with the maintenance cases, demote recruitment to a stated conjecture with a stated test, replace self-confirming predictions with ones that discriminate against entrenchment and ideology accounts), not a new mechanism. One condition on this verdict: it assumes the author accepts that demotion. If the recruitment claim is held to be *the* contribution, the answer to "why does grammar pick out these things" is currently unsupported for every hard case, and the verdict becomes REQUIRES THEORETICAL RECONSTRUCTION.
