# Operator-stratum review synthesis

Date: 2026-08-22
Target: `subprojects/operator-stratum/main.tex`
Reviewers: Claude Fable and Ox Alpha, with clean-room and informed/adjudication passes

## Verdict

The paper has a sound core after reframing. Its defensible contribution is a functional boundary inside grammar: an operator paradigm is a small, closed grammatical paradigm with a role in configuring public update across its ordinary occasions of use. Expressive substance does not determine membership. Tone, gesture, particles, word order, and inflection can all realize operator values.

The revision does not claim that operator membership defines grammaticality as a whole. A community can categorically license or exclude a construction without any operator value being at issue. Public-update role predicts a narrower difference in comprehension and repair locus.

## Context asymmetry

- The clean-room reviewers saw the frozen operator manuscript without the companion formal model. Both found the public-update idea promising but judged its stated extension too broad and its predictions too close to closure-plus-entrenchment.
- The informed Fable pass compared the target with `main.tex` and `section3.tex`. It found direct cross-paper conflicts: opportunity was a membership condition in the main paper; the target used estimator output as a definition; and the two papers assigned incompatible consequences to low-update contrasts.
- Ox Alpha's adjudication saw the manuscript, the companion material, and all earlier reviews. Its targeted follow-up then examined the proposed treatment of complementation, *le hiver*, and *depend of* specifically for level and category errors.
- The final editorial judgment is slightly stricter than Ox Alpha's first adjudication wording. Closed-paradigm entrenchment can explain categorical form correction even when public-update divergence is near zero. Public-update role earns its place only if independently matched contrasts differ in update confusion or repair target.

## Accepted findings

1. Operator membership has two conditions: paradigmatic closure and a paradigm-level public-update role.
2. Opportunity count, competitor strength, and preemption modulate evidence concentration, categoricality, satiation, and stability. They do not define operator membership.
3. Constructional licensing, operator membership, value selection, and exponent licensing are intersecting dimensions.
4. *Le hiver* is a value-intact operator-exponent error. Definite reference is preserved; the construction excludes the unelided determinative before an *h muet* word.
5. *Depend of* is a constructional-licensing error. Complement constructions can be schematic at the category/function level while a lexical head narrows the realization to a PP headed by *on* or *upon*.
6. Historical recruitment is a diachronic support hypothesis. It neither defines synchronic membership nor rescues a failed synchronic test.
7. Individual evidence updating does not produce population exclusion or persistence without adoption, retention, interaction, or transmission dynamics.
8. Projectibility is an analyst-side relation between observed and expected features. It does not cause a speaker's feeling; exposure and read-out processes belong at the speaker level.
9. Signaling-game and simulation results establish what a model can produce, not what actual communities do.
10. The strongest tests concern repair locus, paradigm-level contribution despite token redundancy, and matched cross-substance profiles. Satiation and opportunity effects are corollaries of entrenchment rather than operator-specific tests.

## Rejected or demoted claims

- Rejected: a closed, high-opportunity contrast with no public-update role should be treated as style rather than grammar.
- Rejected: categorical correction, an asterisk, or high opportunity diagnoses operator membership.
- Rejected: *depend of* belongs to the operator stratum merely because its preposition choice is narrow and categorical.
- Rejected: next-turn distributions define public-update role. They are one coarse estimator alongside comprehension probes.
- Rejected: opportunity sets or paradigms themselves police speakers. Speakers correct; opportunity changes their evidence.
- Demoted: public-update work recruits contrasts into grammar. This is a historical hypothesis requiring its own evidence.
- Demoted: protocol headers explain processing. The comparison is retained only as a scope analogy.
- Demoted: resistance to satiation distinguishes operators. It should track evidence concentration for operator and non-operator constructions alike.

## Resulting predictions

1. After closure, opportunity, competitor strength, surprisal, prosody, and task are matched, wrong operator values should produce more update confusion and update-oriented repair than contrasts with no public-update role.
2. Value-intact exponent errors and constructional errors can remain categorically correctable, but repair should concentrate on the form or construction rather than on force, commitment, role, or reference.
3. A token can be locally redundant while its paradigm has a positive expected public-update contribution. A closed, dense paradigm with near-zero contribution across its opportunities is a live falsifier if it nevertheless yields the same update-confusion profile as an operator.
4. Operator-value errors should pattern across segmental, tonal, prosodic, gestural, and nonmanual exponents once opportunity and frequency are matched.

## Formalization decision

No new simulation or Lean development is warranted at this stage. The revised claims require independent empirical measurements rather than a new mathematical result. The existing OVMG formalism can represent paradigm-level public-update divergence and separate update-oriented from form-oriented repair; extending Lean now would add design commitments without establishing the empirical boundary.
