# Adjudication

## 1. Level errors: yes, five identifiable ones

The manuscript and the review layer committed level errors at the following points. Each is an invalid inference across levels, not merely loose wording:

**(L1) Ontic condition defined by an estimator (T:64).** Condition (i) is stated as a world-side membership fact about the community's repertoire, but is defined by an "iff" over next-turn-type distributions — a corpus statistic — and then immediately conceded to be "imperfect." That conflates the **conventional/population level** (which contrasts the community has closed into update-configuring paradigms) with the **evidence level** (how an analyst estimates membership). An imperfect estimator cannot be the definiens of the thing estimated. Corrected: membership is a fact about the repertoire; Δ-style probes and next-turn shifts are diagnostics of it.

**(L2) Analyst-side property as speaker-level cause (T:169, T:175).** "Projectible licensing profile … makes a local clausal mismatch *feel* like a failure" makes an analyst-facing property of the category (what the profile licenses the analyst to infer) the cause of a speaker's felt anomaly. What explains the feeling is the concentration of speakers' evidence — their exposure history — not Goodmanian projectibility, which is a property of projections, not of brains. Relatedly, T:175 uses "projectible" to mean "constraining," a different property entirely.

**(L3) Mereological slip on policing (T:74, T:155).** "Opportunity sets can police wrong values." Opportunity counts have no bearer capable of policing. Policing is behavior by speakers under accountability profiles; what opportunity density does is concentrate the population's evidence, which raises the probability that speakers will detect and correct. The inference from "large N" to "the set polices" skips the actual agents.

**(L4) Speaker evidence presented as population exclusion (T:157 fn).** The footnote moves from a Bayesian inference at the speaker-evidence level ("strength tracks frequency") to population-level non-licensing of \**goed*, with no adoption/retention step. Individual evidence accumulation does not entail repertoire exclusion; the companion's own simulations were built to expose exactly this gap. Population exclusion requires adoption dynamics, not updating alone.

**(L5) Model result as empirical demonstration (T:297).** A signaling-game result is reported as showing what communities do. It shows what the model class can do. Different level, different warrant.

There is also an internal inconsistency that is a level artifact: §2's first misfire treats agreement as a case where "the update couldn't go through" (T:46), while §§6–7 establish that agreement is locally redundant and policed despite intact uptake (T:209, 249). The first misfire should be clause-type, switch-reference, or evidential.

## 2. Category errors: yes, in both directions

The manuscript's tables (strata table, boundary-cases table) invite reading stratum membership as an **exclusive classification**: each case gets one row, one predicted mismatch profile. That is a category error of the first kind — treating constructional licensing, lexical conditioning, operator-value selection, and operator-exponent selection as mutually exclusive bins. They are not. A single token error has coordinates on several independent axes simultaneously:

- **Axis A — constructional licensing:** a community-level fact about which complement/form constructions are licensed, typically schematic and inherited, not stored as atomic collocations.
- **Axis B — operator value:** whether the contrast configures public update (clause type, commitment, scope, reference linking).
- **Axis C — value correctness:** did the speaker select the right grammatical value?
- **Axis D — exponent licensing:** given the value, is the chosen exponent among the licensed forms?

\**Goed* fails D only. \**Depend of* fails A/D while B and C are untouched. Complementizer choice engages A and B together. Collapsing these into "operator vs. payload" hides the fact that most familiar asterisks are D-errors (value-intact exponent errors), which the manuscript itself argues — but then its own tables file them under single strata.

The second kind of category error is the reverse collapse: treating the axes as one class by making "policed as grammar" the definition of operator status. Policing is a *consequence* of A×B interaction under accountability profiles, not a fourth axis and not the membership criterion (this is the detector/target confusion the adjudication already flagged at T:309).

## 3. Coherence of the two-axis treatment

Coherent, with two minimal amendments:

1. **Axis B must be paradigm-level.** As written, the public-update condition is evaluated token-by-token, which makes gender concord — the paper's showcase — fail its own condition (the adjudication's row 5 / M1). State B as the paradigm's *expected* contribution over its opportunities: token-Δ≈0 with paradigm-Δ>0 is then a coherent position, not a falsifier.
2. **Operator-exponent licensing is not a third independent axis.** It is a *dependent relation between C and D*: it exists only where there is a licensed value (C) and a closed exponent set (D), and it is precisely the locus of preemption-driven exclusion. Presenting it as "third" risks re-importing the exclusive-bins error. Call it what it is: the D-axis constraint operating inside paradigms recruited on the A/B axes.

With those fixes, the ontology is: **A** (community licensing, schematic/inherited) ⊗ **B** (paradigm-level update configuration) determine what is policed; **C** and **D** locate where within a licensed construction a given token went wrong; preemption operates on D (and on A-level constructional competitors like \**depend of*).

## 4. Classification

| Case | A: constructional licensing | B: operator value | C/D: error locus |
|---|---|---|---|
| *depend on/upon* | Licensed schematic complement construction, lexically narrowed to PP[on/upon]; inherited, not atomic | No public-update instruction; supplies a required relational complement value | \**depend of*: value and construction intent intact; exponent unlicensed by dense competitor preemption → **pure D-error** |
| *le/l' hiver* | Closed allomorphy rule within determiner/clitic repertoire | Marginal (reference setting; the paper's own "recruited/edge") | Value (definiteness) intact; allomorph misselected → **D-error at expression-shape/operator boundary** |
| *goed/went* | Verb-inflection construction licensed | Tense is an operator; past-time value correctly selected | **Pure D-error**: paradigm integrity violated, update recoverable |
| Turkish harmonic suffix | Suffix paradigm licensed; harmony is a closed form-selection dependency | Depends on suffix function (case/number marking can be operator) | Disharmony with correct grammatical value → **D-error**; disharmony with wrong value → C+D |
| Complementizer selection | Closed complementizer inventory, lexically selected by matrix predicate | **Yes** — *that* vs. *whether* changes commitment presentation and update type | Wrong complementizer for intended value → **C-error** (with B engaged); this is the case where A and B genuinely intersect |
| Case/adposition role marking | Closed case paradigm; adposition government partly lexically conditioned | **Yes where obligatory/exclusionary** (argument linking, participant tracking); payload where the adposition contributes spatial/conceptual content | Wrong case for right role → **D-error**; wrong role assignment → **C-error** |

Note the pattern the classification reveals: the cases the manuscript leans on hardest (*goed*, *le hiver*, *depend of*) are all D-errors, which is exactly why they support the "value-intact but excluded" thesis — and exactly why they cannot be used to define operator membership, since none of them mis-sets an operator value.

## 5. Paragraph for the target paper

> A single surface error can be located on several independent dimensions, and grammaticality talk responds to all of them. First, there is the question of whether the construction itself is licensed in the community's repertoire — usually a schematic pattern, inherited rather than memorized case by case, though particular heads may narrow it sharply (\**depend of* is out even though nothing about the sentence's meaning or force is wrong). Second, there is the question of whether the contrast at issue does public work: whether selecting one of its values rather than another changes what the utterance counts as — a question rather than an assertion, one commitment structure rather than another, one participant tracked as agent rather than patient. Complementizer choice passes this test; the choice between *on* and *upon* does not. Third, given a licensed construction and a grammatical value the speaker intends, there is the question of whether the particular exponent produced is among the forms the paradigm maintains. Most familiar corrections — \**goed*, \**le hiver*, a disharmonic Turkish suffix, \**depend of* — fail only this last test: the intended value is intact and recoverable, but the form is excluded because a competitor reliably wins the relevant opportunities. Keeping these dimensions apart explains why the asterisk is selective without being uniform: it marks failures of constructional licensing, failures of value selection, and failures of exponent selection, and the correction a hearer offers ("you mean *went*", "did you mean *whether*?", "who did what?") reveals which dimension failed.

---

**Summary verdict.** The level errors are real and enumerable (L1–L5 above): the recurring pattern is presenting diagnostics as definitions, analyst-side properties as speaker-level causes, population-level facts as agent behaviors, and speaker evidence as population outcomes. The category error is treating the four relations — constructional licensing, lexical conditioning, operator-value selection, operator-exponent selection — as exclusive classifications when they are orthogonal coordinates, with operator-exponent licensing being a dependent relation (C-under-D), not a third axis. The two-axis proposal is repairable with the paradigm-level amendment to condition (i) and the reclassification of the "third relation"; nothing in it requires new theory.
