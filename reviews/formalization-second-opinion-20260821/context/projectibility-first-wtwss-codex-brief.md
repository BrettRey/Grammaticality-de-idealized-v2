# Projectibility-First Linguistic Categories

## Codex working brief for *Words That Won’t Hold Still*

### Purpose and epistemic status

This document reconstructs the meaningful conceptual work from a long conversation with Brett Reynolds. It is a handoff for Codex instances helping develop *Words That Won’t Hold Still: How Linguistic Categories Work* (WtWSS), related papers, and the proposed countability experiment. It is not a transcript.

Keep four kinds of material sharply separated:

1. **Existing evidence.** The Barner, Wagner, and Snedeker event-nominal experiment and claims already present in Brett’s manuscripts.
2. **A proposed experiment.** The artificial-language *dax–tiv* study has not been run.
3. **A stipulated result.** Parts of the discussion ask what would follow if that experiment produced exactly the predicted pattern. Never report this as an actual result.
4. **A developing theoretical proposal.** The projectibility-first reconstruction and the proposed reorientation of WtWSS remain recommendations, not changes Brett has necessarily adopted.

The central clarification from Brett is decisive: this is **not a general homeostatic property cluster (HPC) theory in new clothing**. It is projectibility-first. HPC, causal-network, constructional, functional, social, and other accounts can explain why particular projections remain secure, but none is built into the basic criterion.

## Executive synthesis

The emerging proposal is:

> A scientific category is an inference-carrying compression. Its appropriate grain is whatever must remain organized for the inference to survive the changes relevant to the claim.

The substantive thesis is:

> Category and attribution unit are jointly selected by transport performance.

The compact operational questions are:

1. What travels?
2. What has to travel together?
3. How far does it travel?
4. What, if anything, keeps it travelling?

The fourth question is explanatory and comes after the first three. This order prevents a slide from stable profile to mechanism, from mechanism to maintenance, and from maintenance to homeostasis.

Two other formulations should be retained:

> Labels don’t carry inferences; organized invariances do.

> Categories are licensed bets. The grain of the bet is whatever must remain together for it to keep paying.

The theory should not require every claim to be written as a fully explicit typed object. The elaborate type ledger is an audit representation for disputed handoffs, appendices, preregistrations, and adversarial review. In ordinary exposition, the four questions above should do nearly all the work.

## 1. The problem the proposal addresses

Scientific and linguistic practice often treats a category label as though it had an invariant bearer and inferential meaning. A word is called *count*, a construction is called *productive*, a sentence is called *grammatical*, or a benchmark score is attributed to a model. Researchers then move from the label to predictions without checking whether the relevant inference survives changes in task, context, population, time, or unit of analysis.

The resulting mistake is not merely overgeneralization. It is often a grain error. The category may support one inference at the lexeme level, another only at the constructional-network level, and still another only at the speaker-by-task level. Asking whether the category is “really” lexical, constructional, cognitive, or social can therefore impose a single ontological answer on several different inferential questions.

The proposal begins with the projection to be licensed, not with a favored unit or mechanism. It asks what observations are supposed to license what further expectations, under which changes. Candidate units are then compared by how well the inference transports.

This yields a useful conservation principle:

> Broader projectibility must be paid for by a larger unit, a richer profile, a weaker conclusion, stronger evidence, or some combination of these.

No label gets unlimited inferential reach for free.

## 2. Minimal conceptual architecture

The following distinctions matter, but Codex should introduce them only where an argument actually turns on them.

### 2.1 Category, profile, and kind-standing

- A **category** is a sorting or coordination device.
- A **profile** is the structured property-and-relation pattern that supports a specified projection.
- A category earns **kind-standing for a field** when tracking that profile licenses sufficiently reliable expectations for that field’s target and loss structure.

A category can remain useful for pedagogy, communication, record keeping, or comparison while losing kind-standing for a particular inferential task. Field relativity therefore does not imply arbitrariness. Support grades, prospective ranges, failure conditions, and demotion rules constrain the claim.

### 2.2 Attribution unit and explanatory bearer

Keep these distinct.

- The **scientific attribution unit** is the unit to which the scientist attributes the category or response profile.
- The **explanatory bearer** is the organized system whose operation explains why the profile occurs or persists.

An abstract construction can be a legitimate attribution unit without being a causal agent. A speaker’s constructional network, learning history, and competitors may be the explanatory bearer. Conversely, an entire speaker or community can permissively possess a capacity even when a narrower organized subsystem carries the immediate explanatory burden.

Do not assume that there is always one uniquely minimal bearer. Evidence may support a set or Pareto frontier of units trading predictive reach against complexity. A claim of unique minimality requires stronger intervention and model-comparison evidence than the countability thought experiment supplies.

### 2.3 Projection range

“Projectible” is incomplete unless the range is stated. Relevant transformations can include:

- trained to untrained constructions;
- one discourse or referential context to another;
- one interlocutor to another;
- immediate performance to delayed retention;
- one speaker sample or population to another;
- one language, period, register, or community to another;
- one measurement procedure or target loss to another.

Projection edges do not compose merely because each local step seems plausible. A handoff from one unit, target, or field to another requires transport evidence. This is as true of linguistic theorizing as of benchmark evaluation.

### 2.4 Security is downstream

Once a profile and its demonstrated range are identified, ask what secures the projection. Possible answers include:

- recurrent statistical structure;
- constructional coverage and competition;
- lexical or event-semantic organization;
- acquisition and entrenchment;
- functional pressure;
- social norms and coordination;
- causal-network organization;
- maintenance mechanisms;
- corrective, perturbation-sensitive homeostatic control.

These are not interchangeable. Homeostasis is the strongest and narrowest claim in this list, not a synonym for stability or projectibility.

## 3. Membership stability and inferential stability

The conversation identified a distinction that should be prominent in WtWSS:

- **Membership drift:** the instances assigned to a category change.
- **Inferential drift:** the consequences licensed by membership change.

A category can remain extensionally crisp while becoming inferentially treacherous. Conversely, graded membership can coexist with reliable projection over a stated range. Boundary sharpness therefore neither guarantees nor is required for projectibility.

A “category wind tunnel” is a useful experimental metaphor: perturb context, competitors, task, interlocutor, time, or population and measure membership judgments and projected consequences separately. Stable membership with unstable consequences is especially diagnostic because ordinary taxonomic measures will miss the failure.

## 4. Existing linguistic evidence: event nominals

Barner, Wagner, and Snedeker (2008) provide a compact demonstration that morphosyntactic category alone does not determine quantity judgments.

Participants compared displays containing six brief events with displays containing two longer events. The central contrasts were approximately:

- *more dances* favored number;
- *more dancing* favored duration;
- *more jumps* favored number;
- *more jumping* also favored number.

Count syntax elicited number-based judgments about 92% of the time for both punctual and durative predicates. With noncount syntax, punctual predicates elicited number-based judgments about 86% of the time, while durative predicates did so only about 15% of the time.

The important result is not just that syntax and semantics interact. *Dancing* and *jumping* are both morphosyntactically noncount, yet their quantity profiles diverge because lexical event structure contributes different individuation resources. For morphology, the nominal construction may be an adequate attribution unit. For the quantity judgment, the evidentially relevant unit is at least the nominal construction together with event structure in a speaker system.

This is a model of the general projectibility-first move: identify the inference, perturb a candidate component, and enlarge the unit only when held-out prediction improves.

## 5. Countability as an ordered projectibility profile

WtWSS already treats countability as structured rather than binary. The relevant diagnostics form an ordered profile.

### Tight diagnostics

These demand relatively exact access to atoms:

- *a(n)*;
- *how many*;
- low exact cardinals.

### Moderate diagnostics

These require individuation but can tolerate more looseness:

- *several*;
- distributive environments.

### Loose diagnostics

These can operate with comparatively weak atom access:

- *many* and *few*;
- high or round numerals;
- plural agreement.

Quasi-count nouns such as *cattle*, *police*, and *clergy* retain substantial loose-count behavior while resisting tight diagnostics. Lexically available alternatives such as *cow* and *officer* can function as anchors that carry exact individuation, allowing the collective form to remain stable in a quasi-count region. This is functional anchoring or offloading, not evidence for a single binary lexical feature.

The label *quasi-count* is useful because it supports an asymmetric prediction: loose diagnostics should be available more readily than exact atom-access diagnostics. The hierarchy earns its keep only if it predicts held-out behavior, learning, or change.

## 6. Proposed artificial-language experiment

### 6.1 Training system

Teach participants a miniature language with novel animal referents and two linked nominal forms.

- Collective *dax*: licensed in patterns such as *many dax*, *hundreds of dax*, and *the dax are*.
- Singulative *tiv*: licensed in patterns such as *a tiv*, *three tivs*, and *each tiv*.

The system initially lets *dax* handle magnitude and collective reference while *tiv* supplies exact individuation.

### 6.2 Experimental manipulation

Cross two factors:

1. **Functional anchor:** *tiv* remains available or is made unavailable.
2. **Referential demand:** participants face magnitude-oriented tasks or exact-reference tasks.

Under exact-reference pressure with *tiv* unavailable, participants must find another route. Possible responses include:

- a partitive such as *three of the dax*;
- an invented classifier or singulative;
- countward reanalysis of *dax*, producing forms such as *three dax*, *a dax*, or an innovative plural.

### 6.3 Measurements

Measure more than trained-item reproduction:

- naturalness judgments across the ordered countability diagnostics;
- production choices;
- agreement and demonstrative completions;
- interpretations of quantity expressions;
- transfer to untrained constructions;
- transfer to new referential contexts;
- transfer to a new interlocutor;
- retention after roughly 24–48 hours.

The critical evidence is transport. A local response in the trained exact-reference task is much weaker than a countward shift that appears across held-out diagnostics and persists after delay.

### 6.4 Critical estimand

The conceptual estimand is the post-training change in tight relative to loose diagnostics in the anchor-removed/exact-demand cell, compared with the corresponding change in the other experimental cells. The analysis should retain participant, item, diagnostic, and task variation rather than reducing the result to a single aggregate percentage.

## 7. Stipulated expected result and its interpretation

For the thought experiment, assume the following result:

- Exact-reference pressure changes *dax* only, or much more strongly, when *tiv* is unavailable.
- The change is concentrated in moderate and tight diagnostics rather than merely increasing already-loose uses.
- It transfers to untrained constructions and referential contexts.
- It appears with a new interlocutor.
- It remains detectable after approximately 48 hours.
- Loose diagnostics remain comparatively stable.

A concise report would be:

> Exact-reference pressure expanded learners’ *dax* countability profiles only when the competing singulative was unavailable, and the expansion travelled across untrained constructions, contexts, interlocutors, and a 48-hour delay.

### 7.1 What this would support

The randomized manipulation would support a causal claim about the effect of functional ecology on learning and generalization. The transport tests would show that the response is not confined to the trained item-task pairing. Any adequate explanation would have to represent the contribution of competitors somehow.

For this learning response, the lexeme alone would be too narrow an explanatory unit. A participant-specific *dax–tiv–constructional* network would be a better candidate because removing *tiv* changes held-out behavior involving *dax*.

### 7.2 What it would not support

Do not infer any of the following from the stipulated result alone:

- that countability is universally or essentially network-level;
- that the experiment has identified a unique minimal cognitive representation;
- that constructions themselves are causal agents;
- that the same result holds in natural English or another natural language;
- that individual adaptation has become community-level change;
- that the effect is diachronically stable;
- that countability is an HPC kind;
- that corrective homeostatic control is present.

The strongest claims would concern experimental causation and within-participant transport. Claims about representation and mechanism would remain abductive; claims about natural languages, communities, and diachrony would remain untested.

## 8. Why the full type ledger was rejected as a presentation format

An earlier attempt explicitly typed nearly every component: field, target, loss, attribution unit, explanatory bearer, profile, transformation range, estimand, security claim, and every transport edge. The result was useful as an audit log but not as a workable public theory.

The repair is analogous to type inference in programming. Most types should remain implicit when context fixes them. Make them explicit at seams where an argument changes:

- the target inference;
- the grain or bearer;
- the population or field;
- the transformation range;
- the strength of the explanatory claim.

The main text should use the pocket questions. The full ledger belongs in an appendix, preregistration, reviewer response, or adversarial audit.

## 9. Anti-vacuity constraints

The proposal becomes vacuous if a failed prediction can always be rescued by refining the category, enlarging the unit, or restricting the range after seeing the data. Impose the following discipline:

1. Candidate grains and transformation ranges should be specified prospectively where possible.
2. Post-hoc category refinement, unit enlargement, or range restriction should incur a complexity penalty.
3. A more elaborate unit must improve held-out prediction, not merely redescribe the training data.
4. Competing grains should be compared directly rather than declared compatible after the fact.
5. Claims should be demoted when transport fails: a category can remain pedagogically or descriptively useful without retaining kind-standing for the failed inference.

This is also the answer to the worry that projectibility-first analysis merely says “use whatever works.” The proposal requires prospective bets, explicit ranges, out-of-sample performance, and penalized comparison among rival units.

## 10. Construction Grammar within the larger framework

Construction Grammar is not one grain, one level, or one field. It is a multigrain research program. Constructionist analyses can range over words, partially schematic constructions, construction families, networks, speaker knowledge, acquisition, processing, variation, and diachrony.

Within the projectibility-first framework, Construction Grammar contributes:

- candidate attribution units;
- structured form–function profiles;
- hypotheses about lexical-constructional interaction;
- mechanisms or organizing relations such as coverage, analogy, competition, and statistical pre-emption;
- predictions about learning and productivity.

Projectibility-first analysis asks which candidate unit supports which inference over which range and whether an alternative grain predicts better. It therefore encompasses Construction Grammar only in the limited meta-theoretical sense that it can evaluate constructionist and non-constructionist grain proposals by a common standard. It does not absorb Construction Grammar as a single layer in a fixed hierarchy.

A useful formulation is:

> Construction Grammar is a multigrain theory of one major source of linguistic projectibility, situated inside a more general theory of which scientific attributions license which inferences.

## 11. Likely Adele Goldberg reaction

This is a reasoned forecast from Goldberg’s published work, not a report of her response.

Goldberg would probably be sympathetic to the empirical substance. The *dax–tiv* result fits constructionist commitments to learned form–function pairings, lexical semantics, contextual interpretation, networks, competition, partial productivity, and statistical pre-emption. The disappearance of a conventional competitor creating pressure for a novel solution is naturally expressible in her framework.

She would probably challenge the novelty claim. A constructionist can say that words and constructions already participate in distributed networks and that productivity depends on coverage, similarity, competition, and communicative function. The projectibility-first account must therefore show that selecting a projective grain generates prospective predictions or discriminates among rival analyses rather than simply redescribing familiar interaction effects.

She might also resist the assumption that there is a unique minimal unit. A graded, distributed network or a set of near-optimal units may fit her framework better. Any statistical-pre-emption interpretation would need to establish that the competitors express sufficiently similar messages in sufficiently similar contexts; mere formal alternation is not enough.

The likely bottom line is enthusiasm for the experiment, sympathy for the network-level interpretation, and skepticism about a large metaphysical sales pitch unless the projective-grain framework adds discriminating predictions.

## 12. Consequences for *Words That Won’t Hold Still*

### 12.1 The conceptual inversion

The current WtWSS architecture is maintenance-first. Its central movement is roughly:

> mechanism → maintained cluster → real category → projectibility

The developing proposal reverses the order:

> projection target → supporting profile → appropriate grain → demonstrated range → securing explanation

This is a substantial rotation, not a demolition. Most empirical examples and much of the anti-essentialist argument survive. The book’s account of why categories deserve scientific authority changes.

WtWSS should no longer claim without qualification that categories are real because they are maintained or that categories are “habits, sustained by mechanisms, projectable because sustained.” Those formulations make maintenance necessary and explanatorily prior. A revised synthesis is:

> Linguistic categories are licensed bets. Their scientific standing depends on which expectations their profiles support, at what grain, and across what range. Constructional, cognitive, social, causal, and sometimes homeostatic organization can explain why those expectations travel.

Peirce consequently moves forward. The interpretant identifies what a category does: it generates a habit of inference. Boyd supplies one powerful account of how some such habits remain reliable, but HPC is no longer half of a universal synthesis.

### 12.2 Claims in the present manuscript that need revision

At least four recurrent claims should be softened or replaced:

1. **Maintenance as the criterion of reality.** Replace this with field-relative kind-standing earned through a projectibility profile. Maintenance can explain or strengthen the standing.
2. **Shared mechanisms as necessary for cross-linguistic category identity.** Different mechanisms can realize sufficiently similar projectibility profiles. Conversely, a shared mechanism need not guarantee that the same inference travels.
3. **Projectibility plus homeostasis as two mandatory diagnostics.** Replace the binary audit with support grades: profile and transport first, then network order, maintenance, and finally corrective control.
4. **Negative definition as automatic disqualification.** Negative categories often project poorly, but the failure must be shown. Institutionally or causally stabilized absences can sometimes support genuine inference.

### 12.3 Implications for the theoretical chapters

The existing Part II contains the right material but in the wrong explanatory order.

- **Chapter 4, “Categories without essences.”** Recast the opening around projectibility profiles and licensed bets. HPC should appear as one family of security explanations, not the general criterion.
- **Chapter 5, “Discrete from continuous.”** Retain the scale-relative account of sharpness, but separate categorical or membership stability from inferential stability. A sharp boundary can enclose a poor projective category.
- **Chapter 6, “The Stabilizers.”** Turn this into a security chapter: which processes order, maintain, or control a profile, and what additional evidential standing does each support?
- **Chapter 7, “Projectibility and the Good Bet.”** This is conceptually the first positive chapter. Its core claims should govern Chapter 4 even if the physical chapter order remains.
- **Chapter 8, “Failure modes.”** Make grain selection central rather than a late repair. Reinterpret thinness as failure of held-out projection, fatness as the pooling of distinct projective profiles, and negativity as a hypothesis to test rather than an automatic verdict. Replace the cumbersome main-text audit with the pocket questions.

A more radical reordering would make the positive sequence: the good bet; the profile; the bearer or projective grain; security from network order through control; discreteness; failure and demotion. Reordering is optional; the explanatory priority is not.

### 12.4 Implications for the case chapters

- **Countability.** Make it the flagship demonstration of jointly selecting category profile and grain. The stipulated experiment would show that a word-level description can suffice for some distributional projections while a competitor network is required for adaptation under exact-reference pressure.
- **Definiteness and deitality.** Keep the distinction among comparative concept, language-particular descriptive category, and projectibility profile. Ask separately which form, interpretation, and discourse expectations travel.
- **Lexical categories.** Do not rank nouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs solely by mechanism density. Compare the inferences each label licenses at lexical, constructional, and cross-linguistic grains.
- **Pro-form gender.** State whether the intended projection concerns individual forms, paradigmatic systems, discourse chains, speakers, or communities. Do not let “system” silently change referents.
- **The category zipper.** Recast it as a multigrain map rather than evidence that one HPC logic is scale-invariant. Constructions are one important family of composite candidate units.
- **Grammaticality itself.** Distinguish the object evaluated from the measurement episode. The attribution unit may be a sentence or construction relative to a speaker, task, context, and time; the explanatory bearer may be a broader processing and social system.
- **Social stabilization.** Use this chapter to specify when evidence warrants enlarging the unit from an individual speaker system to a discourse community or speech community.
- **How categories travel.** This can become the conceptual culmination. “Travel” should mean demonstrated transport across named transformations, not merely historical or metaphorical extension.
- **The gauntlet.** Require prospective ranges, held-out prediction, complexity penalties, and demotion when transport fails. Put the complete type ledger in the diagnostic appendix.

### 12.5 Why the title improves

The title *Words That Won’t Hold Still* gains a second, deeper reading. Words fail to hold still not only because their classifications drift, but because words often are not the units carrying the relevant inference. A lexeme can remain unchanged while its inferential behavior depends on constructions, competitors, speakers, tasks, and communities.

The book therefore becomes less a general claim that linguistic categories are maintained objects and more an account of when linguistic classifications acquire inferential authority.

## 13. A usable WtWSS thesis paragraph

Treat the following as a candidate, not settled wording:

> *Words That Won’t Hold Still* argues that linguistic categories are inference-carrying compressions rather than definitions or mere labels. A category earns scientific standing when partial observation of a structured profile supports further expectations at a stated grain and across a stated range. The relevant unit is whatever must remain organized for those expectations to survive the changes at issue. Constructional, cognitive, social, causal, and sometimes homeostatic processes can explain why the projection remains reliable, but no one kind of mechanism defines kindhood in advance.

## 14. Instructions for future Codex work

When continuing this project:

1. Use **projectibility-first**, not “a full HPC view.”
2. Begin with the target inference and demonstrated transport range.
3. Distinguish the scientific attribution unit from the explanatory bearer when the argument depends on it.
4. Do not treat an abstract construction as a causal agent.
5. Do not claim a unique minimal unit without direct model comparison and intervention evidence.
6. Treat Construction Grammar as a multigrain object-level research program, not a single level contained within a rigid hierarchy.
7. Keep membership stability distinct from inferential stability.
8. Treat mechanisms, maintenance, and homeostasis as progressively stronger security claims, not synonyms.
9. Preserve the distinction between actual evidence, proposed studies, stipulated results, and theoretical consequences.
10. State the demonstrated range explicitly and refuse unsupported jumps from laboratory learners to natural languages, communities, or diachrony.
11. Use the four pocket questions in ordinary prose. Reserve the type ledger for seams and audits.
12. Prevent post-hoc rescue through prospective specification, held-out testing, and complexity penalties.

The shortest reliable handoff is:

> What travels? What has to travel together? How far? What secures it?
