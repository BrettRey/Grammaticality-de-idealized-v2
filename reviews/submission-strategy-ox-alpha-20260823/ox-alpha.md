## Submission-strategy adjudication

**1 & 2. Order and stagger.** Submit **Main to *Linguistics* now**, and hold the companion only until Main clears desk review, then submit it elsewhere. Waiting for a referee decision buys nothing: desk review is where any overlap objection surfaces, referees at different journals won't coordinate, and waiting for acceptance could cost a year or more for zero risk reduction. Simultaneous submission to different journals is legal and ethical; the only real exposure is editorial optics, which the cover-letter distinction below handles.

**3. Rankings.**

- **Main:** 1. *Linguistics* (general-relevance scope, no word ceiling, diamond OA, preprint-friendly). 2. *Journal of Linguistics* Research Article (fits the 15k ceiling and all-branches theoretical remit, but its one-paper-per-author rule makes it unusable while the companion circulates). 3. *Glossa* (admissible fallback, though fit is generic).
- **Companion:** 1. *Language Sciences* (explicitly welcomes metatheoretical debate; the "social technology" section gives it genuine transdisciplinary texture). 2. *Glossa*. 3. *Journal of Linguistics* Research Article once Main is resolved. Not the "Looking Back, Moving Forward" category: that format examines an established notion's trajectory; this paper advances a new position. Not *Journal of Pragmatics*: the attested-data requirement isn't met.

**4. Companion venue and query.** *Language Sciences*, ordinary research article. Yes, send a presubmission query: the paper has already been desk rejected once purely on scope, so scope is its demonstrated failure mode, and a 150-word query costs a week and eliminates most of that risk. Do not revise before querying unless an editor asks.

**5. Independence.** Cross-citation of public preprints helps but doesn't by itself preempt a salami-slicing suspicion, because shared examples remain visible. Cover-letter language:

> These papers share a framework and occasional examples because they were developed together, but they ask different questions and neither depends on the other's results. The longer paper asks what grammaticality claims amount to generally and how population licensing profiles arise and change; the companion isolates one boundary within that framework, namely which contrasts are policed as grammar rather than accent or word choice, and derives diagnostics specific to that boundary. Each cites the other's public preprint, and each is intelligible without it.

**6. Strongest objection and tripwire.** The strongest reason against the staggered-concurrent plan is that an editor or reviewer judges the pair as one program split in two, contaminating both reviews. Observable trigger: any desk rejection or first-round comment that cites redundancy, overlap, or "companion piece" fragmentation rather than fit or substance. On that event, stop, let Main reach a decision, and either merge the companion's diagnostics section into Main's revision or shelve the companion until Main is accepted. A scope-only rejection, by contrast, changes nothing about sequencing.

**Calendar.**

1. **This week:** Submit Main to *Linguistics*. Same day, send the presubmission query to *Language Sciences* for the companion.
2. **On Main passing desk review** (expect 2–4 weeks): submit the companion to *Language Sciences* (if the query drew a positive or neutral reply) or straight to *Glossa* (if silent past three weeks).
3. **On Main's first referee decision:** reassess only if the redundancy tripwire fired; otherwise do nothing.
4. **Whichever paper is rejected on substance first** becomes the *Journal of Linguistics* candidate once the other is past review, since the one-at-a-time rule will then be satisfied.
5. **Hold all new submissions** until at least one first decision is in hand.
