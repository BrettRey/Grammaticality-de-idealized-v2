**3. Query discipline.** A presubmission query is appropriate even where a journal does not invite one, provided it is short (~150 words), asks only about fit, and states plainly that submission is not contingent on the reply. Editors treat such queries as routine; none penalize them. But my decision rule changes: **silence is non-evidence, not a negative answer.** Editorial offices triage queries unevenly, and non-reply carries no information about the manuscript or its prospects. So: send the query in week 0; submit to *Language Sciences* on schedule around week 3 whether or not anyone has replied; act only on explicit replies. A positive reply supplies calibration and possibly an editor's name for the cover letter. An explicit negative saves a desk cycle. Silence means proceed exactly as planned. My earlier instruction to divert to *Glossa* after three silent weeks wrongly converted non-response into evidence.

## Submission sequence

1. **Week 0:** Submit Main to *Linguistics*. Same week, send the 150-word scope query for the companion to *Language Sciences*.
2. **Weeks 2--4 (Main clears desk):** submit the companion to *Language Sciences*, regardless of query silence.
3. **Thereafter:** hold all new submissions until a first decision arrives, unless the tripwire fires; a scope-only rejection changes nothing about sequencing.
