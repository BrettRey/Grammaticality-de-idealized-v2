# Answer: the stagger is withdrawn

I concede. Walking through each candidate benefit against the challenge:

**1. "Main establishes the framework record first."** Already false as a reason. Both papers are public LingBuzz preprints, reciprocally cited, with cover letters disclosing the relationship. Journal acceptance adds no record that citation and disclosure do not already establish on day one. This benefit is produced now, by the preprints, and waiting produces zero increment.

**2. Desk decision as quality signal.** A *Linguistics* desk rejection would not license inference about the companion's prospects at *Language Sciences*: different editors, different scope criteria, and desk decisions turn on fit and framing, not framework soundness. A desk acceptance likewise transmits nothing the companion can use. Neither branch of the outcome changes the operator submission, which is exactly the test the challenge imposes. The stagger fails it.

**3. Early substantive warning.** The only causal channel with any content: if Main draws desk-level comments flagging a defect in the shared framework, Brett could repair before submitting the companion. But desk stages rarely generate substantive comments, the preprints already expose the framework to public scrutiny on the same timescale, and the existing redundancy tripwire (any overlap or fragmentation complaint pauses the companion) catches the real risk where it actually surfaces, at review. A 2–4 week hedge against a low-probability, mostly-covered event does not purchase its cost.

**4. Sequencing hygiene at the fallbacks.** Parallel submission creates no JL conflict: the constraint "never both manuscripts at JL at once" is a rule about ladders, not a reason to delay entry into them. It binds identically whether submissions are staggered or simultaneous.

So: no material benefit survives. The stagger rested on a record-establishing function that journal submission never had, given public preprints. **Withdraw it. Submit both in week 0**, with the independence paragraph and mutual disclosure intact.

What still controls the decision: reciprocal citation and disclosure from day one; the redundancy tripwire; the JL exclusivity rule on the fallback ladders; no revision before an editor asks. Those do the work the stagger pretended to do, without the delay.
