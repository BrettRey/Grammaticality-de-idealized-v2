# Submission-strategy adjudication

Act as a senior general-linguistics editor and publication strategist. Evaluate
the two attached manuscripts as they actually stand. This is not a prose review
and not an invitation to propose more revisions unless a specific revision is
necessary for venue fit. Give an independent recommendation about submission
order, timing, and venues.

## Manuscripts

1. **Main:** *Grammaticality de-idealized*. Roughly 11,200 words and 31 pages
   once the attached reader-facing model section is included. It gives the broad
   Operator-Value Model of Grammaticality: separate structural coverage,
   operator compatibility, saturation, community licensing, processing cost,
   and evidence; then dynamics and discriminating predictions. All mathematics
   is in a separate optional supplement. A LingBuzz preprint is public. The
   current intended first venue is *Linguistics: An Interdisciplinary Journal of
   the Language Sciences*.

2. **Companion:** *Why clause structure is judged like tense and agreement:
   public-update operators and grammaticality*. Roughly 7,100 words and 20
   pages. It isolates a narrower thesis: an operator paradigm has a small closed
   conventional inventory and a paradigm-level public-update role. It separates
   operator membership from constructional licensing, value choice, and
   exponent licensing, and proposes repair-locus, token-redundancy, and
   cross-substance tests. A LingBuzz preprint is public. An earlier version was
   desk rejected by *Functions of Language* solely as outside scope; there was no
   referee report. It has since been theoretically and expository revised.

The papers cite one another's public preprints. They share the public-update
idea and some examples, but the Main paper's object is grammaticality in general
and its population dynamics; the companion's object is the narrower functional
boundary and empirical diagnostics of operator paradigms.

## Current venue facts (checked against official pages on 2026-08-23)

- *Linguistics* publishes questions of current or general relevance about
  language as communicative, cognitive, social, or historical, across grammar,
  meaning, use, communities, synchrony/diachrony, and qualitative/quantitative
  methods. It is diamond open access at no cost to authors. It accepts anonymous
  self-citation and public preprints. Its current journal instructions do not
  state a research-article word ceiling.
- *Journal of Linguistics* covers all branches of theoretical linguistics. Its
  Research Articles and “Looking Back, Moving Forward” articles have a 15,000
  word ceiling. The latter examine a notion, concept, assumption, or theoretical
  position and its current/future role. The journal considers only one paper by
  a given author or co-author at a time. It is gold open access, with agreements
  and waiver routes rather than a guarantee of zero author cost.
- *Journal of Pragmatics* requires engagement with current pragmatic theory
  **and attested language data** from languages/cultures. That is not what the
  companion presently offers.
- *Language Sciences* explicitly welcomes radical/transdisciplinary work and
  metatheoretical, epistemological, and axiological debate, with a current
  emphasis on language/languaging as coordinative, affiliative, and
  integrational activity.
- *Glossa* is a broad general-linguistics fallback; both papers fit its 15,000
  word ceiling, but venue fit rather than mere admissibility is the question.

## Decisions to adjudicate

1. Should Main be submitted first, the companion first, or both at roughly the
   same time to different journals? Distinguish legal/ethical permissibility
   from editorial optics and intellectual strategy.
2. If staggered, should the author wait only for Main to clear desk review, wait
   for a first referee decision, or wait for acceptance? State why.
3. Rank the best three venues for each manuscript as currently written. You may
   name a better venue not listed above, but do not invent policy facts.
4. For the companion in particular, choose between ordinary *Journal of
   Linguistics* Research Article, its “Looking Back, Moving Forward” category,
   *Language Sciences*, *Glossa*, or another venue. Say whether an editorial
   presubmission query would materially reduce risk.
5. Does reciprocal citation of the public preprints adequately establish
   independence? Give a two- or three-sentence cover-letter distinction that
   would neutralize a redundant-publication or salami-slicing concern without
   sounding defensive.
6. Identify the strongest reason **against** your preferred plan and the
   observable event that should trigger a change of plan.

Be decisive. End with a calendar-like sequence of concrete submission actions.
Do not give acceptance-probability numerology. Do not assume that prestige
dominates fit, access, or time-to-feedback.
