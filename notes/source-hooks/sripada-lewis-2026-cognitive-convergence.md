**Source:** Sripada, C., & Lewis, R. 2026. Cognitive convergence: Deep similarities between
large language models and human cognition. arXiv:2607.26179, v1 (28 Jul 2026). Central note:
`literature/sripada-lewis-2026-cognitive-convergence.notes.md`.

**Why this project cares.** NOTES.md flags Powell's *Contingency and Convergence* as a priority
frame (are grammatical categories convergent functional solutions?). S&L §5 gives that frame
named, citable machinery on the AI side: **contravariance** (Cao & Yamins 2024, *Cogn. Syst.
Res.* 85:101200 ~-- harder tasks narrow the space of viable solutions, so independently trained
systems converge) and **architectural canalization** (their coinage, Waddington via Ariew 1996
~-- shared architecture biases which solutions emerge within that narrowed space). The pairing
is a template for arguing convergence *with a mechanism* rather than from resemblance lists,
which is what a Powell-style claim about grammatical categories would also need.

**What to watch.** Their convergence evidence is representational-similarity work of the family
the calibration critiques (Gröger et al.) have unsettled; cite the mechanisms as proposals, not
the alignment results as settled.
