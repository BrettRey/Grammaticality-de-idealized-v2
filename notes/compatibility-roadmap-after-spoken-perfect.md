# After the spoken perfect

I would close the first round on the English perfect and put the next research session into a human learning study. The first round changed the manuscript. We have a spoken example with *finish*, evidence for what the speaker dated, and two analyses that give the same event reading. The listener calculation shows that even a change in which day’s rule people apply can come from shifting attention. Reopen the reserved weekdays when two accounts predict different answers under an independently supported assumption about attachment or topic shift. The current cabinet experiment also earns a pause. At 256 independent judgments its planned test detects even full use of the disclosed information only 12% of the time under the specified model. ## 1. Start where there is already human behaviour to explain

Conwell and Snedeker taught 24 adults a miniature language with two word orders. In one condition, verb meaning helped predict which order occurred. The same learners described events, judged sentences and chose which of two videos a sentence described. With unfamiliar verbs, the semantic cue helped production and judgments. Comprehension was high in both conditions. Some comprehension trials reversed who did what to whom in the foil. Those trials matter here because choosing a video on the strength of a familiar noun tells us less about interpretation of the construction. [Study, tasks and results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11588301/)

Give this dataset one session. List which training distributions can be reconstructed, which responses can be linked to a person and verb, and which counts survive the published aggregation. Preserve productions in the other order, lexical errors and omitted responses wherever the records allow. The authors supply data and code on reasonable request. The published tables can establish feasibility while access to individual responses is unresolved. The first deliverable is a table of observable contrasts and one prediction worth making.

## 2. Ask each learner to predict the same unfamiliar verb

Give the models the same scenes, verb meanings and exposure distributions. Compare an OVMG implementation that learns which verb–construction combinations are available, a constructional learner that generalizes graded associations across semantically related verbs, and a simple predictor using verb class and frequency. Specify how each produces a description, an acceptability response and a scene choice. Give the categorical account an explicit way to interpret an unfamiliar order and calibrate that part on familiar items. A separate free parameter for every test cell would remove the reason to compare the models.

Before fitting, identify one contrast across tasks that the models predict differently. For example, if a learner rarely produces an unfamiliar order, what does each model predict about recovering the agent from it when the video choices reverse the roles? First check that the materials actually present that combination. Some comprehension cells may be at ceiling. Published patterns already consulted count as development evidence.

## 3. Let the result determine the next investment

- **The models agree on all available responses.** Record the shared prediction and the assumption responsible. Propose one additional observation only if it would change that prediction. A larger sample cannot break an exact equivalence.
- **The predictions differ.** Generate data from each candidate at the actual participant and item counts, fit every candidate, and record how often the comparison selects the wrong account or remains inconclusive. Carry a recoverable difference to one fresh verb set, condition or independent dataset with predictions fixed in advance.
- **The human comparison favours one account and it predicts fresh items.** Take that account and the best simpler rival into English. The dative alternation offers a concrete test of preference and availability. After a speaker freely chooses *give the book to Kim*, what do they do when the context invites *give Kim the book*? Record production, paraphrase and avoidance on eligible occasions. Bresnan and Ford provide a benchmark for contextual preferences. Examine repair or confidence once the rivals predict how those responses should differ. [English dative study](https://web.stanford.edu/~bresnan/86.1.bresnan.pdf)

The paper-sized outcome is a model that learns from a specified history, predicts production and interpretation on fresh human material, and states which claims about grammatical organization those observations support. An exact limit or a simpler model winning earns a place in that result.
