# Reconstruction plan: why some interpretable deviations count as ungrammatical
## 1. Governing decision
This paper will answer the question it began with:

> Why do speakers classify some readily interpretable deviations as errors of grammar, while classifying others as lexical, stylistic, phonological, orthographic, pragmatic, or social faults?

The repair-centred version reversed the explanatory order. Repair is a possible held-out consequence of an answer to this question. It is not the explanandum. The earlier plan assigning selective grammaticality to the old LingBuzz preprint is superseded.

The paper remains primarily theoretical. Projective declarations and failure conditions discipline the explanation; they do not commit the present article to a funded participant study.
## 2. Revised answer
The paper will propose that grammatical fault attribution is {==**system**==}{>>does this just push the question back to "what's a system"? Are vowels systems? Is apologizing or information packaging?<<}{id="c1" by="user" at="2026-08-24T15:32:55.125Z"}**-tracking** but fallible. Speakers tend to classify a deviation as grammatical when they locate the trouble in a conventional system for assembling form--value relations, rather than in the selected lexical content, pronunciation, spelling, discourse fit, style, or social act. Whether they do so depends on a graded profile that can be assessed independently of the attribution itself.

The bearer is a **grammatical choice system in a community**: for example, an English past-tense system, a switch-reference system, or a complement-licensing relation. The **grammatical-choice profile** records properties of that bearer, including:

- recurrence at a specifiable morphosyntactic or constructional choice site;

- boundedness and contrastive organization of the alternatives;

- organization by a language-specific semantic or relational frame;

- conventional mapping between values, structural functions, and exponents;

- strength and distribution of selection or omission constraints;

- concentration of the pattern across relevant speakers and situations;

- depth and position within a constructional licensing chain; and

- degree of conventional contribution to the utterance's public update.


These are profile coordinates, not a checklist of individually necessary and jointly sufficient properties. A strict paradigm in the sense developed by Nørgård-Sørensen and Heltoft occupies a strong region of this space; it does not define the extension of grammar. Constructional licensing supplies a second route, and a single token can implicate several levels with different profiles.

The resulting explanation is not “speakers call grammatical whatever belongs to grammar.” Profile placement must use production distribution, substitution, omission, semantic contrast, and constructional analysis fixed without consulting the fault labels, corrections, or repair outcomes to be predicted. The projection from that placement to a later attribution can therefore fail.
## 3. What public-update function contributes
Public-update contribution is one central property in the profile, not the full explanation and not a membership condition. It matters most for a subset of systems—clause type, polarity, tense anchoring, evidentiality, person, switch reference, and related contrasts—whose values configure what update an utterance proposes and how interlocutors are to take it.

The information-theoretic intuition is correspondingly limited but important. A marker may carry little unexpected token information and be fully recoverable, yet still select among a small conventional repertoire of update instructions. Recoverability and surprisal therefore cannot by themselves explain why its misuse is attributed to grammar or why a wrong value has different consequences from a wrong exponent.

The manuscript will not claim, without evidence, that public-update function causes systems to arise, makes them stable, maintains them, or subjects them to corrective control. Instead, it will state narrower projections to entrenchment, cross-situational non-negotiability, update confusion, and repair locus. Matched update-neutral grammar—declension class, concord, and comparable exponent restrictions—will be the principal control. If public-update profile adds nothing under that comparison, the operator component is demoted while the broader system-tracking account can survive.
## 4. How the central cases work
### Wrong operator value
A wrong switch-reference value changes a conventional update instruction even when the hearer recovers the intended reference. Its system has a strong paradigm profile and a high public-update profile. The account predicts grammatical fault attribution and, separately, update-directed consequences.
### _Goed_
_Goed_ selects the PAST value and communicates it transparently, but violates a community-specific mapping between that value and the exponent permitted for _go_. The fault targets exponence within a strongly structured grammatical system. This case shows why public-update difference cannot define grammaticality and why interpretive recovery does not erase grammatical fault attribution.
### _Depend of_
This is a layered boundary case, not a nuisance to be assigned wholesale to grammar or lexicon. Complementation selects among a small set of functions; functions constrain phrasal categories; a PP complement introduces an item-restricted choice of preposition. The higher links have strong grammatical profiles, while the last link resembles lexical collocation.

The analysis is admissible only if the licensing chain is specified before attribution evidence is examined. An element counts as structurally selected when it realizes a function, category, or item choice governed by the construction's licensing relations—not merely because it often co-occurs with the construction. This yields an ordinal expectation: function-selection and category-selection violations should attract more consistently grammatical attribution than restricted-preposition errors, which should in turn differ from open collocational oddity. Mixed and shifting responses to _depend of_ would then be predicted rather than dismissed.
### Phonology, orthography, style, and social conduct
Closedness cannot demarcate grammar by itself. {==Phonological==}{>>a point I made early on in the paper's history is that grammar is *primarily* a form–meaning pairing. That's one of its central properties. Phonology doesn't (typically) pair with meaning.<<}{id="c2" by="user" at="2026-08-24T15:36:28.049Z"} and orthographic systems are also conventional and constrained; stylistic and social systems can also be stable and heavily sanctioned. The paper will treat them as neighboring systems and ask why their faults are normally assigned to different domains. Their distinct structural bearers and relations to lexical identity, written practice, discourse choice, and social action are part of the source profile, not exceptions stipulated after the outcome.

School-taught shibboleths provide a crucial dissociation. Institutional transmission can cause people to apply a grammar label where the independently measured system profile is weak. The theory therefore predicts a difference between spontaneous, unprimed fault localization and explicit labels elicited under normative or classroom framing. A socially caused grammar label need not identify a grammatical target correctly.
## 5. Heltoft and Nørgård-Sørensen
They are the central architectural predecessors, not one row in a novelty list. The paper will inherit:

- alternatives organized as a paradigm rather than collected extensionally;

- recurrence in a syntagmatic domain;

- a language-specific semantic frame;

- the extension of paradigmatic organization beyond inflectional morphology;

- connecting paradigms and paradigmatization where the textual evidence supports those attributions.


The paper will not inherit:

- strict paradigmhood as a definition of all grammar;

- a classical necessary-and-sufficient category theory;

- an inference from paradigmatic organization to stability, causal order, maintenance, or corrective control; or

- a requirement that partial, defective, changing, or construction-sized systems be excluded.


This is adoption as a structural scaffold within a projectibility-first theory of categories. The manuscript must distinguish what their work already provides from the new projection targets and failure rules.
## 6. Projective declarations
These are theoretical declarations to be refined before any empirical test. The numerical tolerances should be fixed only for a concrete dataset and design; the article should not manufacture universal cut-offs.
### P1. Selective fault attribution
**Source.** A system-in-community receives a grammatical-choice profile using production, substitution, omission, semantic-frame, and licensing-chain evidence that excludes the later attribution outcome.

**Target.** Higher profile values warrant an expectation of more consistent grammar-directed fault attribution, relative to matched lexical, stylistic, phonological, orthographic, pragmatic, and social deviations.

**Bearer and population.** Particular choice systems in a specified community; attributions sampled from speakers of that community.

**Conditions.** Deviations are interpretable; task framing, processing load, frequency, schooling, and sanction are matched or separately modeled.

**Scope and timescale.** Held-out tokens, speakers, and situations within the declared community and period. Cross-linguistic or diachronic extension requires a new declaration.

**Minimum useful reach.** A pre-specified directional or rank-order improvement over rival predictors and matched controls, not merely successful redescription of the analyzed examples.

**Warrant plan.** Converging production/distributional analysis followed by held-out fault-domain coding; rival models include frequency, interpretability, processing, discursive secondary status, schooling, and sanction.

**Revision rule.** Systematic failure demotes or splits the profile. Failed cases cannot be rescued by redefining their community, level, or domain after the outcomes are seen. Any narrower fallback must be declared in advance.
### P2. Mixed and moving edges
**Source.** A system has a layered or intermediate profile—particularly lexical item restriction embedded in a grammatical licensing chain—or its production concentration and selection constraints are changing.

**Target.** Fault attribution should be more variable across speakers and situations and should migrate across grammar, lexicon, and style as the source profile changes. _Depend of_ is the synchronic boundary case; _who/whom_ and irrealis _were_ are candidate diachronic cases, subject to source verification.

**Bearer, population, and conditions.** A specified system-in-community compared across declared speaker groups, situations, or cohorts without normative priming.

**Scope and timescale.** Synchronic variation or a named historical interval; these are separate uses and must not be pooled opportunistically.

**Minimum useful reach.** The independently ordered licensing layers or measured change must predict the direction of attribution variation.

**Warrant and revision.** Production and historical evidence fixes the source ordering; held-out attribution evidence tests it. If institutional transmission better predicts persistent grammar labels after production concentration collapses, it becomes a rival cause of the read-out rather than a post-hoc membership property.
### P3. Operator-specific consequence
**Source.** Systems are matched on the structural coordinates of their grammatical-choice profiles but differ in independently assessed public-update contribution; errors are separately classified as wrong value or value-preserving wrong exponent.

**Target.** Higher public-update contribution warrants expecting a larger difference between wrong-value and wrong-exponent cases in update confusion, update-directed correction, and repair locus.

**Bearer, population, and conditions.** Matched systems and error tokens in a declared community, with opportunity, constructional licensing, processing, frequency, schooling, and sanction accounted for.

**Scope and timescale.** Held-out systems and tokens in the declared study. No automatic historical or cross-linguistic projection.

**Minimum useful reach.** Incremental predictive value beyond the general grammatical-choice profile and rival predictors.

**Warrant and revision.** Profile placement and outcome coding are frozen before testing. A null matched result demotes public-update contribution from explanatory property to descriptive correlate for this target; it does not redefine the systems or make repair the criterion of membership.

## 8. Manuscript reconstruction
This will be a reconstruction of the best earlier argument using the stronger literature, distinctions, and projectibility discipline now assembled. It will not revert the source and will not add a new introduction to the repair-centred paper.

1. **Title and abstract.** Put selective grammaticality in the title and opening sentence. State the system-tracking/profile answer and its limits. Mention repair only as one downstream projection.

2. **Opening contrasts.** Use a wrong operator value, _goed_, _depend of_, and matched phonological, orthographic, stylistic, and social cases. The cases must expose the theory to failure rather than merely illustrate categories already assigned.

3. **The explanandum.** Separate interpretability, acceptability, grammatical status, fault-domain attribution, and response cause. Give the source- ambiguity literature its proper role.

4. **Projectibility before ontology.** Introduce the bearer, profile, P1, and the non-circular placement rule before extended paradigm or mechanism discussion.

5. **Paradigm architecture.** Present Heltoft and Nørgård-Sørensen as the closest structural predecessor. Distinguish inheritance, relaxation, and new projection targets. Position Boye and Harder as a genuine rival predictor.

6. **Layered grammatical choice.** Analyze value, exponent, and constructional licensing without forcing every case into the operator subset. Work through _goed_ and the _depend of_ licensing chain.

7. **The public-update coordinate.** Present its information-theoretic motivation, operator cases, update-neutral grammatical controls, and P3. Do not infer stability or enforcement from function.

8. **Neighboring systems and causes.** Treat phonology, orthography, lexicon, style, pragmatics, social sanction, and institutional teaching as comparisons, not a miscellaneous residue.

9. **Variation and change.** Use P2 to explain gradation and moving edges without making the category arbitrary or rescuing failed projections.

10. **Predecessor comparison.** Organize the table by explanatory job: system identification, response cause, fault-domain target, public-update role, and held-out projection. State what the proposal inherits before stating the residual contribution.

11. **Testing without displacing theory.** Present P1–P3, controls, and failure rules as an empirical programme. Corpus or experimental pilots remain future work unless suitable evidence is actually obtained.

12. **Conclusion.** Answer the opening question directly. Then state what would make that answer fail.

## 10. Working title and reader contract
### Working title
> **Why some interpretable deviations count as ungrammatical**
### Reader contract
> This paper asks why speakers classify some readily interpretable deviations as grammatical faults and others as lexical, stylistic, phonological, orthographic, pragmatic, or social faults. It proposes that these attributions imperfectly track graded profiles of community-specific systems for assembling form--value relations. Strict paradigms and constructional licensing identify strong and layered regions of that profile space; public-update contribution is one central property that licenses narrower predictions about the consequences of wrong-value errors. By fixing system profiles independently of the judgments and repairs to be explained, the account yields fallible, non-circular projections for selective attribution, boundary variation, and repair locus.
## 11. Decision requested
The central decision is whether **system-tracking via a graded grammatical-choice profile** captures the intended answer. The consequential commitments are:

- public-update function is important but not membership-fixing;

- strict paradigms are a strong structural case, not a classical definition;

- _depend of_ is analyzed as a pre-specified layered licensing chain;

- grammaticality can be graded within the grammatical domain, with a real but moving and sometimes epistemically inaccessible edge; and

- the paper's theoretical value is cashed out in P1–P3, not made conditional on running a participant study now.


If those commitments are right, this is the architecture to implement in the LaTeX.
