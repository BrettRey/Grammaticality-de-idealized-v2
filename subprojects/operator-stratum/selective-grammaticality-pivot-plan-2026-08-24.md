# Restoring the paper's question: selective grammaticality
## Decision
The current repair-centred framing is a wrong turn. It treats the paper's best empirical consequence as though it were the explanandum. This paper is meant to answer the broader question:

> Why do speakers classify some readily interpretable deviations as errors of grammar, while classifying others as errors or differences of wording, style, pronunciation, pragmatics, orthography, or social conduct?

The earlier framing plan assigned that question to the old LingBuzz preprint and restricted this paper to predicting repair locus. That division of labour is now superseded. The present paper will own the selective-grammaticality question. Repair will provide held-out evidence about a proposed answer; it will not replace the question.
## The explanatory answer to develop
The paper should defend a layered answer rather than claim that public-update profile is a necessary-and-sufficient definition of grammar.

1. {==**Grammatical fault attribution targets a conventional system of constrained form--value choices**==}{>>in a projectibility-first framework.<<}{id="c1" by="user" at="2026-08-24T15:11:55.373Z"}**.** A deviation is a candidate for grammatical classification when it violates a community-specific choice system: a value contrast, the permitted exponent of a value, or a licensed constructional relation. This distinguishes the target of the classification from the many causes of a negative response, including processing difficulty, schooling, stigma, frequency, and task demands.

2. **Paradigm structure makes some choices non-negotiable parts of the code.** Here the paper should adopt Nørgård-Sørensen and Heltoft's account of {==strict paradigms==}{>>to the extent that these are compatible with my view of categories<<}{id="c2" by="user" at="2026-08-24T15:12:33.523Z"} as its principal structural scaffold: bounded alternatives recur in a syntagmatic domain and are organized by a language-specific semantic frame. 3. **Public-update function** {==**explains**==}{>>fully? unlikely, but it's one central property among a cluster of properties<<}{id="c3" by="user" at="2026-08-24T15:13:05.365Z"} **why a particularly important subset of those systems is treated as architecturally central.** Clause type, tense, evidentiality, polarity, person, switch reference, and related systems help configure what public update an utterance proposes and how it is to be taken. Their communicative contribution is therefore not exhausted by token surprisal or recoverability. This is the information-theoretic intuition in disciplined form: a locally redundant signal may still select among a small conventional repertoire of consequential update instructions.

## What the paper must distinguish
The rewrite will keep the following variables separate throughout:

| Question | Object to identify | Evidence that must not be collapsed into it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is there a grammatical system? | Paradigm or constructional-licensing structure | Asterisk, correction, or low acceptability alone |
| What went wrong? | Value, exponent, construction, lexical choice, style, stance, social act, etc. | The cause or intensity of the response |
| Why did a person respond negatively? | Processing, exposure, schooling, sanction, frequency, task, or other causes | The linguistic target they name or repair |
| How much does the system configure public update? | Independently coded operator profile | Later comprehension or repair outcomes |
| What follows if the account is right? | Fault-domain attribution, comprehension pattern, and repair locus | The criteria used to place the item in advance |

The source-ambiguity literature bears directly on the third row. Coseriu, functional grammar, grammaticalization, interactional grammar, sociolinguistics, and repair research each constrain other rows. The contribution is not that these literatures failed to notice distinctions; it is the attempt to make their objects commensurable in one non-circular explanatory architecture.
## Claims to make, and claims not to make
### Governing claim
Speakers' selective attribution of interpretable deviance to grammar is not explained by incomprehensibility, low probability, prescriptive sanction, or social conventionality alone. It becomes intelligible when we distinguish violations of conventional grammatical choice systems from other sources and targets of trouble, and when we recognize that public-update function makes a subset of those systems especially consequential.
### Novelty claim
The novelty is architectural and predictive. The paper brings paradigm identification, constructional licensing, public-update profile, response cause, and fault-domain attribution into a single staged account. It then asks whether independently identified differences predict held-out classifications and repair patterns. It does not claim to have discovered paradigms, discourse-functional grammar, interactional accountability, or the grammar--acceptability distinction.
### Claims to avoid
- Public-update function defines grammar.

- Closed paradigms are coextensive with grammar.

- Repair reveals grammatical ontology directly.

- All asterisks have the same target or cause.

- Information theory can discover grammatical membership without a prior language-specific analysis.

- The existing literature has simply ignored the relevant distinctions.

## How the {==empirical programme==}{>>If we can do an empirical aspect, that's nice, but my focus is theoretical.<<}{id="c8" by="user" at="2026-08-24T15:21:29.591Z"} changes
The present profile-by-error-locus repair interaction remains useful, but it cannot be the sole confirmatory target if the paper promises to explain selective grammaticality. The programme needs two linked outcomes:

1. **Fault-domain attribution:** Given an interpretable deviation, what do speakers take to be wrong---grammatical value, grammatical form, construction, lexical content, style, pronunciation/orthography, pragmatic fit, or social act?

2. **Consequence:** Conditional on that attribution, what confusion, correction, reinterpretation, sanction, or repair follows?


The strongest design freezes the linguistic analysis and the outcome codebook before observing those outcomes. A within-paradigm comparison of wrong-value and value-preserving exponent errors tests a key internal distinction. Matched constructional, lexical, stylistic, and social cases test whether the account actually explains selective grammaticality rather than only variation within grammar.

The paper may remain a theoretical proposal if no participant study is presently fundable. Corpus repair, metalinguistic correction, usage guides, learner corpora, and public acceptability datasets can provide pilot evidence, but the manuscript must label each result according to what it can establish. A corpus of corrections is not, by itself, a clean sample of community grammar classifications.
## Failure conditions
The account must be allowed to lose.

- If independently diagnosed paradigm and constructional membership do not help predict grammatical fault attribution beyond frequency, processing load, schooling, and sanction, the proposed account of selective grammaticality fails.

- If public-update profile adds no held-out information about the consequences of value errors, the operator-profile component fails even if the general choice-system account survives.

- If the distinction between value, exponent, and construction cannot be coded independently and reliably, the central empirical contrast is not identified.

- If low-profile open lexical or stylistic alternatives attract the same stable grammatical classifications under matched conditions, the proposed boundary is wrong or incomplete.

## Portfolio relation
The old LingBuzz preprint records an earlier stage of the argument; it does not own the question in perpetuity. The OVMG paper supplies the population-level architecture for coverage, compatibility, saturation, licensing, evidence, read-out, and change. This paper should sit upstream of that machinery by asking what kind of linguistic target a grammaticality classification purports to pick out, and why that target differs from lexical, stylistic, pragmatic, and social fault domains. The two papers should be complementary without requiring readers to accept one in order to understand the other.
## Recommended title and reader contract
Working title:

> **Why some interpretable deviations count as ungrammatical**

Working reader contract:

> This paper explains the selectivity of grammatical fault attribution by distinguishing grammatical choice systems from the causes of negative responses and from neighbouring fault domains. It argues that paradigm structure and constructional licensing identify the relevant systems, while a graded public-update profile explains why a central subset has especially strong interpretive and interactional consequences. The proposal is tested by predictions about fault-domain classification and the different treatment of value, exponent, and constructional errors.
