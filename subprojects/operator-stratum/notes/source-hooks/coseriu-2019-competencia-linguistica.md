## 1. The paper's one negative claim about Coseriu holds

Before 2026-09-27, `operator-stratum.tex` said Coseriu's distinctions "don't settle on their own"
which contribution makes a norm grammatical, a bounded negative licensed without the primary text.
The primary text shows why: he merges lexical, grammatical, and phonic correctness on purpose. The
paragraph in §3.1 (`sec:practice`) was revised the same day to cite p. 39 directly. Manuscript
locations below are given by section label.

> "Para el juicio de lo correcto, a veces, en el campo léxico se habla también de *propiedad e
> impropiedad*, mientras que, sobre todo, en la gramática y en lo fónico, se habla de *corrección*.
> Podemos considerar, simplemente, la propiedad o impropiedad léxicas como forma particular de la
> corrección y de la incorrección. No hablaremos aquí de *propiedad*, hablaremos siempre de
> *corrección idiomática* y de lo *correcto*." (p. 39, img)

'For the judgment of the correct, one sometimes speaks in the lexical field of *propriety and
impropriety*, while above all in grammar and in the phonic domain one speaks of *correctness*. We
can simply regard lexical propriety or impropriety as a particular form of correctness and
incorrectness. We won't speak of *propriety* here; we'll always speak of *idiomatic correctness*.'

So the residual question is real, and it can now be stated positively with a page number rather
than as a bounded negative.

## 2. What the book already does that the paper claims or needs

**Levels and judgments (the triad the paper cites secondhand).** Three cultural levels with three
kinds of knowledge and three judgments of conformity: universal *saber elocucional* (congruent /
incongruent), historical *saber idiomático* (correct / incorrect), individual *saber expresivo*
(appropriate / inappropriate), over a psychophysical level (normal / abnormal). Figures 1–3,
pp. 32, 35, 38; definitions p. 33 (img); summary pp. 60–61 (ocr). The saber idiomático is "saber
hablar de acuerdo con la tradición lingüística de tal comunidad" (p. 33, img).

**Misclassification as the book's organizing problem.** Ch. I ends by naming two kinds of
confusion (p. 29, ocr). *Vertical* confusions mistake correctness for other judgments of speech:
other linguistic judgments (appropriateness; clarity and comprehensibility, which "no se refieren a
la corrección y ni siquiera a una lengua en particular, sino al hablar en general") and
non-linguistic ones (efficacy, logic, truth). *Horizontal* confusions reduce the correct to the
exemplary or the reverse (p. 30). Ch. II diagnoses "extralimitaciones": qualities of speaking in
general or of discourse are attributed to the language, so "se exige mucho más que la conformidad
con la lengua" (p. 57, ocr). This is the paper's selective-classification problem one level up:
Coseriu separates correctness from its neighbours; the paper asks which correctness is grammatical.

**The asterisk and semantic anomaly** (bears on §5 and on `asterisk-de-idealized`).

> "De suerte que estas expresiones, que en los libros de lingüística moderna se encuentran con
> asterisco, como construcciones agramaticales, no son, en la mayoría de los casos, expresiones
> efectivamente incorrectas." (p. 37, img)

His cases are incongruent, not incorrect: *Los cinco continentes son cuatro*, *El cuerno izquierdo
del unicornio es negro*, *He puesto a cocinar el piano*. The diagnostic is the listener's
response: nobody says "No se dice así en español"; they say "Está usted loco" (p. 37). Footnote 77
(p. 101, img) treats *ideas verdes incoloras que duermen furiosamente* the same way.

**Correct is not well-formed** (bears on the public-targets paragraphs in `sec:practice`, derivability vs public licensing).

> "En algunas formas recientes de la gramática, lo correcto se confunde con lo bien formado.
> [...] lo correcto no es un hecho de competencia [...] sino un hecho de ejecución, de
> performance, esto es, de realización en el discurso. Por lo tanto, lo bien formado de por sí no
> es ni correcto ni incorrecto." (p. 95, ocr)

Hence "una confusión decir que la gramática [...] tiene que enumerar o construir todas las
oraciones correctas de una lengua" (p. 96, ocr). Example: *estuve hablando* is well formed but
incorrect where Spanish requires *estaba hablando* for the content intended (p. 96).

**Correction presupposes comprehension** (bears on "readily interpretable deviations", title and
abstract).

> "para establecer que algo es correcto o incorrecto hay que comprenderlo." (p. 97, ocr)

A teacher who sees *árbol* can correct it only on recognizing that *árboles* was meant (the same
point and example on p. 25, ch. I). "Lo
perfectamente comprensible puede ser incorrecto, pero en ciertos casos lo perfectamente correcto
puede resultar incomprensible" (p. 97, ocr; Gabelentz's centre-embedded German in fn. 74).

**Suspension: the judgment tracks an attributed deficit** (bears on target attribution in `sec:problem` and `sec:levels`, and on
`sec:practice`, "the absence of correction doesn't remove it").

> "Este es el principio general de suspensión genérica: se suspende el juicio cuando no se supone
> que hay deficiencia en el saber." (p. 99, img)

A native speaker who says *estirar la pata* for *morir* is judged inappropriate, not incorrect; a
foreigner who says it is told "Ha aprendido usted una expresión incorrecta" (p. 99). *Haiga* is an
error from a speaker presumed not to know the higher level and a deliberate choice from one who
does: "los mismos hechos pueden considerarse como errores o como no errores" (p. 99, img). The
same logic for incongruence: "Todas las veces que juzgamos algo simplemente como incongruente es
porque no atribuimos la incongruencia a una intención, sino que la atribuimos a un no saber"
(p. 101, img). Specific suspension runs upward: a rule of the language suspends incongruence
(*vaso de agua*, *No he visto nada*), and appropriateness suspends both (metaphor, quotation,
imitation, foreigner talk and baby talk) (pp. 100–103; Figure 8, p. 100). Tolerance differs from
suspension: errors in great writers "quedan incorrectas" and are forgiven for other values (p. 104,
ocr). This is the closest precedent for the paper's separation of fault from response and for
attribution as the response-side variable. Cited in `sec:levels` and `sec:method`.

**Pre-emption** (bears on *goed*, `sec:lexical-cases`, which the Fable review called thin).

> "*en lo nuevo es correcto todo aquello que, correspondiendo a una necesidad expresiva, está de
> acuerdo con el sistema y no se opone a ninguna norma establecida.*" (p. 94, img, italics his)

*Volvimiento* is well formed by the system (cf. *revolvimiento*), but used simply for 'vuelta' it
"es incorrecta, porque, estando de acuerdo con el sistema, se opone a una norma establecida,
porque para esto se dice *vuelta*" (p. 94, img). That is the *goed* / *went* structure, stated as a
general condition. Related: *mögöröb* draws "No es palabra española" and no question about
meaning; *llambada* draws "¿qué significa?" (p. 94, img), another response-type diagnostic.

**Phonology reclassified as grammar** (bears on French elision, `sec:french-case`).

Spanish *le* for *les*: "si se tratara solo de un hecho de fonética, sería una incoherencia [...];
pero parece que, para ciertos hablantes, por lo menos, ya no es un hecho simplemente de fonética
sino de morfología, un hecho funcional" (pp. 89–90, img). French liaison [z] has become a plural
morpheme for some speakers (*quatre [z] officiers*), and in *Vous êtes Italiens* [z] vs *Vous êtes
Italien* "Aparentemente, una norma de *liaison*; pero, en realidad, se trata de una nueva norma
gramatical" (p. 90, fn. 67, img). This is the paper's move for *le hiver*, made on a neighbouring
French phenomenon.

**Correctness is not statistical** (bears on `sec:practice`). The objection that errors
outnumber correct forms is a paralogism: "para cada norma debe haber por lo menos dos posibilidades
de desviación"; what marks an error is inconstancy, and "cuando se hace constante y significa un
solo tipo de desviación con respecto a otra norma, se trata de otra norma" (pp. 96–97, ocr).

**Thin correctness** (bears on `sec:practice`). "Correcto es algo simplemente formal, la conformidad
misma [...] Lo correcto no tiene ninguna propiedad" (p. 61, ocr).

**Scoped repertoire = lengua funcional** (bears on every "scoped repertoire"). A functional
language is syntopic, synstratic, and synphasic (one dialect, level, and style); it is "el concepto
básico de la descripción gramatical", and "También la corrección debería considerarse idealmente
desde el punto de vista de la realización de tal lengua funcional" (pp. 74–75, ocr). Discourses mix
functional languages point by point (p. 75).

**Social incorrectness is not idiomatic incorrectness** (bears on the register/honorific case and
Table `tab:token-failures`). "Yo puedo haberle dicho a esta señora todo lo que le dije de forma muy correcta
idiomáticamente y, a pesar de eso, puedo haber estado muy incorrecto socialmente" (p. 47, img).

## 3. Where the paper differs (adjudicate as *deliberately distinguished*)

- **Idiolects.** Coseriu denies individual languages and calls "correcto en mi idiolecto" senseless;
  correctness requires comparison "con algo que debe ser intersubjetivo" (p. 98, img). The paper
  treats idiolect-relative and model-relative status as legitimate targets distinct from public
  status (`sec:practice`; DECISIONS 2026-08-24). The paper's *public* target matches his; the
  pluralism about targets is its own.
- **Intersubjectivity is not majority agreement.** Pardo Llibrer (2021) paraphrases the sixth
  property as depending on a majority of speakers agreeing on what is correct. The primary text
  (p. 98) makes it a claim about a shared language, against idiolect-relative correctness. No
  conflict with the paper's "majority preference ... don't decide" (`sec:practice`). Don't cite Pardo for
  this point.
- **Dichotomy.** Conformity judgments admit no intermediate values; only deviation (and
  excellence) has degrees (pp. 62–63, ocr). Compatible with the operator paper's token
  conformity; relevant to the OVMG parent's graded status (see §5).
- **Grain.** Coseriu locates correctness in the pairing of expression and content within a
  functional language's structure (pp. 79–80, Figure 6) but doesn't ask which pairings are
  grammatical; p. 39 is the explicit statement. Scope of this negative: chs. I (less pp. 21–28),
  II, and IV read in full, ch. III pp. 74–81 read, and the rest of chs. III and V searched in the
  OCR for *gramátic-*, *léxic-*, *fónic-*, *propiedad*, *morfolog-*, *sintax-* (2026-09-27); the
  hits concern teaching grammar and exemplary varieties, not the grammar–lexicon question. That is the paper's contribution, together with the explanatory-level
  separation and the held-out predictions.

## 4. Where the manuscript now uses the book

Before this reading the manuscript credited Coseriu only for the correctness / appropriateness
split. On Brett's decision of 2026-09-27 it also cites him for the separation of a failure of
correctness from the judgment of it (`sec:levels`); recognition as a condition on correction,
tolerance, well-formed versus correct, and the idiolect disagreement (`sec:practice`); asterisked
incongruence (`sec:repairability`); the *le*/*les* and liaison reclassifications
(`sec:french-case`); the innovation condition at *goed* (`sec:lexical-cases`); and presumed
competence as a response cause (`sec:method`). The paper's own claims are those in §3, last
bullet: which correctness is grammatical, the separation of explanatory levels, and the held-out
predictions.
