# Redrafting the operator-profile paper

## Recommendation

Draft it in stages, but not as independently authored sections. One lead writer
should control the entire manuscript. Subagents should test source fidelity,
worked examples, construct separation, and reader uptake at defined checkpoints;
they should not contribute prose that is pasted into the paper.

The efficient sequence is neither “rewrite everything at once” nor “polish one
section at a time.” First establish a short argument spine, then draft the
dependent theoretical sections, then write the introduction and conclusion around
the argument that actually survived.

## Load-bearing assumptions

| Assumption | What would show it is wrong? | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| The paper is a Glossa research article, not a review article or preregistration. | The literature map or protocol remains the main payoff after worked analyses are drafted. | Narrow the paper or change article type before polishing. |
| Public-update profile predicts something beyond semantic reconstructability and discursive secondary status. | No worked case yields divergent predictions, or the predictors cannot be separated prospectively. | Do not proceed with the operator-specific explanatory claim. |
| Paradigm value and exponent can be distinguished in an independently defensible comparison set. | Plausible exponent controls repeatedly change the value or construction as well. | Narrow the eligible paradigm set; do not manufacture controls. |
| Profile and repair locus are related but non-identical constructs. | Profiling requires the same trouble responses later coded as outcomes. | Downgrade the proposal to a descriptive association or redesign the evidence. |
| Abrusán can be represented fairly without becoming the paper's governing framework. | Her predictor is forced onto cases outside semantic type conflict, or the paper implies that a null effect refutes her theory. | Mark those cases not applicable and treat her account as bounded. |

These assumptions are tested before a manuscript-wide prose pass. Two or three
worked analyses are the decisive checkpoint: if they do not establish separable
predictions, elegant exposition cannot rescue the paper.

## Drafting sequence

### Stage 1: Freeze the argument contract

Produce three compact artifacts before rewriting sections:

1. A one-page argument stating the question, proposed answer, explanatory
   levels, bridge principle, sole prediction, scope, and defeat condition.
2. A two-tier case matrix:
   - confirmatory core: wrong paradigm value versus value-preserving exponent;
   - boundary anchors: constructional exclusion, locally diagnosable semantic
     anomaly, and stylistic or social deviation.
3. A scope-fair predictor table for Abrusán, Boye–Harder, and public-update
   profile, including each account's original explanandum and inapplicable cases.

Do not proceed if the bridge principle cannot be stated without referring to the
observed repair outcome.

### Stage 2: Build the structural skeleton

Reorder the existing manuscript under these headings before polishing prose:

1. The selective-classification problem
2. Prior constraints and live alternatives
3. The cross-level architecture
4. Public-update profiles
5. Worked comparisons
6. Incremental predictor comparison
7. Held-out test and defeat conditions
8. Conclusion

Material that does not earn a place in this argument goes into a holding file,
not into an ever-expanding background section.

### Stage 3: Draft the theoretical core

Draft in dependency order rather than publication order:

1. **Cross-level architecture:** conditioned repertoire; paradigm,
   construction, value, exponent, and response target.
2. **Public-update profile:** four components, bridge principle, construct
   separation, and the profile-by-mismatch-locus prediction.
3. **Worked comparisons:** at least one high-profile paradigm, one hard or
   lower-profile case, and the boundary anchors.
4. **Incremental predictor comparison:** what Abrusán, Boye–Harder, and the
   operator profile each predict, and where each is inapplicable.

This is the first major checkpoint. If the worked cases do not produce distinct,
defensible predictions, reconsider the paper rather than persisting with the
planned structure.

### Stage 4: Draft the surrounding argument

After the core works:

1. Draft the prior-constraints section. Give Abrusán the first and most exact
   reconstruction, but organize the section by argumentative role rather than
   as an author-by-author survey.
2. Compress the protocol to the identification logic needed for the theoretical
   claim: independent paradigm identification, frozen profiles and rivals,
   eligible mismatch pairs, held-out outcomes, the sole interaction, and the
   distinction between defeat and non-identification.
3. Write the introduction so that the core pair, hypothesis, scope, and failure
   condition all appear on the first two pages.
4. Write the conclusion, then the abstract, last.

Detailed scaling rules, covariate inventories, and design simulations should be
retained only if they clarify identification; otherwise move them to an appendix
or the later empirical paper.

### Stage 5: Whole-manuscript integration

Run one reader-facing pass across the complete draft. Normalize terminology,
remove repeated originality defences, verify that every example retains the same
classification, and make the question–answer chain visible at section openings
and closings. Only after this pass should we optimize sentences locally.

## Subagent roles

Subagents should be used as independent checks, not coauthors of sections. The
named checkpoints below are the minimum, not a rigid schedule. During drafting,
the lead writer should also request focused spot checks whenever a consequential
judgment would benefit from an independent view---for example, whether a case is
eligible, whether a source has been characterized fairly, whether two constructs
have begun to collapse, or whether a local revision has displaced the paper's
centre. Routine drafting does not require an agent ritual.

1. **Source-fidelity auditor:** after the prior-constraints section, check every
   claim about Abrusán, Coseriu, Poulsen, Heltoft, and Boye–Harder against the
   local sources.
2. **Case and construct auditor:** after the theoretical core, try to break the
   value–exponent distinction, identify profile/outcome leakage, and test whether
   the worked examples really separate the predictors.
3. **Naive Glossa reader:** after the complete draft, report the perceived
   question, answer, principal predecessor, contribution, and strongest reason
   for rejection before seeing our intended summary.
4. **Adversarial reviewer:** only after revisions from the naive read, conduct a
   final scope, novelty, and identification audit.

The lead writer adjudicates all reports and makes every edit. Agents should not
divide the manuscript by section, because the central distinctions recur across
the whole paper and will drift if several writers instantiate them independently.
Use them regularly where judgment is load-bearing, but not slavishly where the
next step is mechanical or already settled.

## Checkpoints

1. **Now:** approve the drafting method and argument-level outline.
2. **After Stage 1:** inspect the one-page argument, case matrix, and predictor
   table before restructuring the manuscript.
3. **After Stage 3:** decide whether the worked predictions justify continuing,
   narrowing, or abandoning the operator-specific account.
4. **After Stage 5:** review the first coherent full draft, not a succession of
   locally polished fragments.

The practical answer is therefore: draft one coherent argument in dependency
order, not the paper all at once; use Roughdraft at the argument and full-draft
checkpoints; and use subagents to falsify specific decisions rather than to
generate parallel prose.
