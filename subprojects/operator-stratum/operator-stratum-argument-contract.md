# Argument contract for the operator-profile paper
## 1. The argument in one page
### Question
Why are readily interpretable departures assigned to different fault domains? A respondent may treat a departure as the wrong grammatical value, a bad form for the intended value, an excluded construction, semantic anomaly, stylistic misalignment, or socially objectionable conduct. Existing work explains important parts of this allocation without yet supplying one cross-domain predictor.

{==Abrusán and colleagues offer the nearest direct answer for a bounded domain==}{>>we need a clear example where this is insufficient<<}{id="c2" by="user" at="2026-08-24T20:52:16.530Z"}. Where semantic composition fails, locally diagnosable conflicts may leave a predication counterfactually reconstructable, whereas abstract conflicts in constitutive types may prevent such reconstruction. This can distinguish semantic anomaly from a restricted class of semantically driven ungrammaticality. Coseriu establishes that linguistic evaluation has multiple targets. Hofmeister and Poulsen show why a negative response does not transparently identify either its cause or the stable conventional system behind it. Boye and Harder supply a serious alternative criterion for grammatical status in discursive secondary status.

The remaining question is narrower than a general theory of grammaticality but broader than Abrusán's semantic-composition problem: {==can a property established before the response predict where later trouble will be directed across grammatical paradigms and neighbouring fault domains?==}{>>the phrasing seems needlessly complex<<}{id="c1" by="user" at="2026-08-24T20:50:21.504Z"}
### Proposed answer
The proposed predictor is an identified paradigm's conventional **public-update profile**: the degree to which changing its value changes the utterance's move type, commitment holder or source, discourse-participant or referent continuity, or response relevance. Paradigmhood and profile are separate. Paradigms are admitted through a conditioned-repertoire analysis of their bounded alternatives, recurring domain, semantic frame, and obligatory value selection. The profile is then estimated without negative judgments, repair initiations, or later responses.

The explanation is cross-level:

[ \text{paradigm profile};\times;\text{token mismatch locus} ;\longrightarrow;\text{response target}. ]

The paradigm bears the profile. The token bears a wrong-value or value-preserving-exponent mismatch. The response supplies the held-out target of repair. Response causes such as processing, schooling, or social sanction remain separate from all three.
### Bridge principle
A high-profile paradigm value conventionally supplies evidence about how a contribution is to be entered into the public record. Changing that value can therefore alter or unsettle the licensed update, whereas changing only its exponent can leave the intended update intact. The defeasible bridge is **minimal adequate repair**: when interlocutors detect a mismatch, can recover the intended alternative, and are oriented to restoring mutual understanding, they tend to target the lowest level whose correction would settle the contribution. They should therefore target update value when the public record remains in doubt and realization when the intended update is already recoverable. Because higher profiles make alternative values diverge more strongly in the public record, they should increase this relative probabilistic difference between mismatch types.

This bridge assumes that both mismatch types are detectable, that the intended value of the exponent mismatch is independently recoverable, that participants share the conditioned repertoire and have an opportunity to respond, and that update trouble is not completely accommodated or overridden by sanction. It also assumes that response targets can be diagnosed without forcing compound responses into one bin. These are eligibility or design conditions, not consequences inferred from the outcome. A major counter-pressure is recoverability: a highly conventional system may make the intended value so easy to reconstruct that a recipient silently accommodates it. Token-level update uncertainty or conflict must therefore remain explicit, and the claimed relation is predictive rather than automatically causal.

The minimum increase and uncertainty rule must be fixed prospectively. A null, saturated, or reversed relation is possible; profile strength does not make overt repair logically necessary.
### Scope and defeat
The confirmatory population contains conditioned community repertoires with independently identified paradigms for which both mismatch types are genuinely available. Constructional, semantic-anomaly, and stylistic or social cases are boundary anchors, not additional matched conditions. They show what the operator hypothesis does not classify by itself.

Abrusán-inspired semantic reconstructability is coded only where an independent semantic type or composition conflict makes the account applicable. Cases outside that domain are **not applicable**, not low-scoring counterexamples. Boye--Harder diagnostics are imported as a possible correlated predictor of the response pattern, not treated as if their theory already predicts next-turn repair.

In an adequately powered identifying study, the operator-specific answer fails if the frozen profile does not predict the held-out value-versus-exponent repair difference after the pre-specified semantic-reconstructability and discursive- secondary-status comparisons. Collinear predictors, too few independent paradigms, or an unavailable exponent control make the study non-identifying rather than supportive. Failure leaves the paradigm descriptions and component profiles available as descriptive analyses; it removes the claimed explanatory attribution.
### What this does not explain
The hypothesis does not define grammar generally, explain every asterisk, derive Abrusán's type theory, predict whether every semantic anomaly is recoverable, reduce speech acts to context change, or explain why a community sanctions a style or social act. It proposes one independently testable contribution to the broader allocation problem.
## 2. Two-tier comparison set
The first two rows form the confirmatory matched contrast. The remaining rows are boundary anchors with different units and applicability conditions. They must not be described as five mutually exclusive natural classes or as five matched experimental conditions.

| Tier and locus | Independent admission criterion | What is preserved or changed? | Abrusán | Boye--Harder | Operator profile | Candidate response target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Core: wrong paradigm value** | The paradigm is independently identified; context or grammatical dependency fixes intended value (v_i); the token selects (v_s\neq v_i) through an exponent licensed for (v_s) elsewhere in the same paradigm | The exponent is well formed, but the grammatical value and potentially the public update change; constructional exclusion is ruled out independently | Applicable only if the mismatch independently creates a semantic type/composition conflict | Informative about the marker's status; no original direct repair prediction | Directly applicable | Update clarification, accommodation, or repair; form-only correction remains possible and counts against the hypothesis |
| **Core: value-preserving exponent mismatch** | Independent evidence shows (v_s=v_i), but exponent (e^*) is not licensed for that value in construction (C); replacing it with a licensed exponent preserves update, content, argument structure, and construction | Intended grammatical value and update are preserved; (e^*) does not conventionally select a competitor value | Usually not applicable; applicable only if the exponent also creates an independently diagnosed semantic conflict | Informative about the containing grammatical value or construction; the nonce exponent itself has no conventional status to diagnose | Directly applicable as the within-paradigm comparator | Form-directed correction; update repair remains possible and counts against the predicted differential |
| **Anchor: content-preserving constructional licensing failure** | No semantic type conflict or independently identified paradigm value changes; the constructional relation is excluded despite recoverable content | Intended content may survive; constructional licensing fails | Not applicable by definition; a type-driven exclusion belongs in the semantic row | Limited or not applicable unless the construction contains a candidate conventional grammatical expression | Not applicable unless a paradigm value is independently involved, in which case the item belongs in the core | Constructional recast or form correction |

| **Anchor: nonparadigmatic sociostylistic or indexical mismatch** | Propositional content and public-update value are independently preserved; the resource does not clear the paradigm criteria; the form indexes a mismatched style, persona, relationship, or stance | Social meaning changes while the targeted grammatical update need not | Not applicable absent a semantic type conflict | Possibly relevant only where a discrete expression has discursive-secondary status; otherwise not applicable | Not applicable by anchor definition | Stance negotiation, person-directed sanction, style correction, or tolerance |

Social-act violations such as slurs belong in the paper's scope discussion, not in predictor adjudication: none of the three accounts directly predicts their sanction. Register, honorific, politeness, or T/V systems do not automatically belong in the sociostylistic anchor. If they clear the paradigm criteria, they enter the profiled comparison and may provide especially useful low-profile or mixed cases. Compound responses should remain compound rather than being forced into one bin.

| **Public-update profile** | Proposed explanation of one part of cross-domain target allocation | An independently identified paradigm in a conditioned repertoire, combined with token mismatch locus | Frozen effects on move type, commitment/source, discourse continuity, and response relevance | The wrong-value minus exponent difference in update-directed repair increases with profile strength | Nonparadigm anchors and paradigms lacking a genuine value-preserving exponent comparator | A positive identifying interaction supports added prediction; an adequate null defeats the operator-specific attribution |

Source ambiguity, Poulsen's stable-knowledge distinction, and Coseriu's fault domains constrain the identification problem but are not forced into this table as if they were competing scalar predictors.
## 4. Decisions required before restructuring
1. Identify at least two paradigms for which the wrong-value and value-preserving-exponent contrast can be defended without changing the construction or intended value.

2. Identify which register, honorific, politeness, or T/V systems belong in the paradigm comparison and which genuinely remain nonparadigmatic anchors.

3. Determine whether semantic reconstructability can be coded reliably enough to function as a rival predictor in its applicable subset, or should instead remain a bounded theoretical comparison.

4. Confirm that public-update profiling can be performed without using the same response behaviour later coded as the outcome.


If decisions 1 or 4 fail, the operator-specific account should be narrowed or abandoned before the manuscript is restructured.
