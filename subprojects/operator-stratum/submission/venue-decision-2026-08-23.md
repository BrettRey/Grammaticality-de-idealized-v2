## Journal-Reader Contract

> This manuscript changes the debate over selective grammaticality for readers of *Theoretical Linguistics* by treating Heltoft's paradigm-and-interaction analyses as the closest foundation, then asking whether a separately measured public-update profile predicts a held-out repair-locus contrast that constructional, exponent, discursive-status, accent, and lexical explanations do not.

- [x] The contribution is journal-local: it is a broad, contestable theoretical proposal
  designed to elicit responses across formal syntax/semantics, functional grammar,
  pragmatics, typology, and interaction research.
- [x] The expected reader can tell why the paper belongs here without relying on a cover letter.
- [x] Reader's vocabulary decided. For this readership, **free** terms include *operator,
  value, paradigm, clause type, illocutionary force, common ground, speech act,

## Risk Test

- Strongest desk-rejection risk: target-article capacity is tiny, and the editors may not
  find the proposal broad, mature, or controversial enough to organize an issue around.
- Strongest reviewer-rejection risk: the paradigm/profile architecture may be read as
  relabelling Heltoft's and the Danish grammar tradition's existing conjunction. The
  manuscript now concedes that extensional precedent and stakes its residual contribution
  on reclassification plus the held-out repair-locus prediction.
- Strongest "not motivated / no live problem" risk: low, but the paper must explicitly
  enter the current dispute over context change, speech acts, interactional syntax, and
  categorical judgments rather than relying only on foundational sources.
- Strongest "this is an opinion piece / no evidence" risk: the tests are proposed rather
  than executed. The target-article format can accommodate a conceptual intervention,
  but the comparative case table and falsifiers must do real evidential work.
- Strongest "wrong literature / wrong methodology" risk: the present draft does not yet
  engage Wiltschko (2025), the associated *Theoretical Linguistics* commentaries, or
  Levinson (2026), and therefore does not yet distinguish its proposal from the venue's
  most immediate conversation.
