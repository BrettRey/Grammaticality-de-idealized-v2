# Reframing for *Linguistics and Philosophy*

## Recommendation

Retarget the paper to *Linguistics and Philosophy*, but treat this as a
reconstruction around a constitutive claim, not as a change of title or emphasis.
The paper should add a logically prior question to its existing distinction between
source attribution and target classification: **status constitution** asks what
makes the relevant relation a grammatical norm whether or not a respondent labels
the token correctly. The paper should answer its initiating question directly:

> An interpretable token is grammatically defective relative to a scoped
> repertoire when it fails a socially sustained norm that organizes how
> linguistic forms realize or combine semantic, relational, or structural values
> in a recurrent construction. Interpretability, semantic or pragmatic
> consequence, and public response can each come apart from that status.

This is stronger than the present claim that an independently measured profile
should predict grammar-directed attribution. Prediction remains important, but as
an evidential constraint on the proposed ontology rather than the philosophical
centre of the account.

The proposed reframing is promising because the existing manuscript already
contains the needed distinctions. It is not yet ready to support the new claim,
because it does not fully distinguish the worldly norm from the profile used to
measure it, and it sometimes treats a contextually inappropriate grammatical value
as if it were itself an ungrammaticality.

## What to adopt, modify, and reject from the external review

### Adopt

- Make norm-relative token status and explanatory levels the organizing claims.
- Distinguish norm architecture, token conformity or failure, semantic and
  pragmatic consequence, public response, and epistemic access.
- Give linguistic norms an explicit but modest ontology.
- Separate constitutive, functional, semantic/pragmatic, causal, and epistemic
  explanation.
- Treat semantic repairability as an account of one important boundary rather
  than either the general answer or a minor rival.
- Reduce the unexecuted Study A/Study B programme by about two-thirds.
- Remove OVMG as required vocabulary and remove *operator stratum* altogether.
- Make discourse consequences a secondary worked application.
- Retitle the article
  **Why some interpretable deviations count as ungrammatical: linguistic norms
  and levels of explanation**.

### Modify

1. **Do not make a “token analysis” the bearer of grammaticality.** An analysis is
   a theoretical representation. The worldly bearer is a token's conformity to a
   scoped conventional relation. An analyst may represent that relation and the
   token in a formal grammar, constructional analysis, or profile, but derivability
   in one model is neither necessary nor sufficient for community status.

2. **Do not call the grammatical-choice profile the norm.** The norm is a
   socially and historically situated relation in linguistic practice. The profile
   is an evidential model of that relation's constructional embedding, choice
   architecture, and community licensing. It can be mistaken.

3. **Do not present the four substantive levels as a simple causal pipeline.** At
   token time, norm architecture constrains token status; status can have semantic
   or pragmatic consequences; and those consequences can help cause a response.
   Processing, task, schooling, stigma, and authority can also affect the response
   directly. Across longer periods, recurrent responses can help maintain or alter
   a norm. That diachronic feedback does not make a token-level response the
   truth-maker of the norm it reacts to.

4. **Use the proposed norm taxonomy as our analysis, not as an attribution to
   Coseriu.** Coseriu grounds the distinctions among system, customary norm, and
   speech, and among idiomatic correctness and situational appropriateness. The
   three-way distinction below is a further development:

   - first-order assembly norms govern recurrent relations by which linguistic
     expressions and values are combined or realized;
   - second-order usage norms govern which licensed repertoire, register, style,
     dialect, or persona is fitting in a situation;
   - institutional metalinguistic norms govern what schools, editors, or prestige
     institutions call correct grammar.

5. **Replace a unitary public-update state with a plural consequence profile.**
   Bary distinguishes common belief, commitment, and real performative change;
   Simons argues that information available for interpretation need not be common
   ground at all. The paper need not decide between them. It should track a vector
   of discourse consequences without assuming one public record. A provisional
   name is *discourse-consequence profile*; *update-role profile* is a usable
   alternative but less explicit about the plurality.

6. **Distinguish form formation from form--value assembly.** “Token formation” is
   too broad because phonotactic norms help determine possible linguistic forms.
   Pure phonotactics belongs to the constitution of expression shape: it constrains
   syllables, segments, or possible word forms without organizing a contrast in
   semantic, relational, or structural value at a constructional site. A
   phonological process participates in a grammatical norm only when a repertoire
   recruits it into such a relation, as when tone realizes tense or conditioned
   allomorphy realizes a value in a determiner or inflectional construction. The
   distinction is functional rather than a ban on phonological substance.

### Reject

- Do not turn the paper into a general theory of social normativity. It needs only
  a thin ontology adequate to distinguish grammatical from lexical,
  orthographic, situational, and institutional norms.
- Do not imply that every licensed grammatical value becomes ungrammatical when
  it is false, misleading, unintended, or contextually inapt.
- Do not claim that held-out prediction by itself supplies constitutive
  explanation. It checks whether the proposed organization has non-trivial reach.
- Do not add a long survey of recent *Linguistics and Philosophy* articles merely
  to advertise venue fit.

## Load-bearing assumptions and failure conditions

| Assumption | What the manuscript must show | What would defeat the reframing |
|---|---|---|
| Assembly norms distinguish grammar from neighbouring domains | A recurrent constructional relation can be identified without using grammaticality labels, and its role differs from lexical choice, graphic representation, situational fit, and social conduct | “Assembly norm” merely renames whatever speakers call grammar |
| Assembly is narrower than form formation | The account distinguishes grammatical assembly from phonotactics, lexical sign identity, prosodic packaging, and orthographic representation even though all contribute to linguistic tokens | Any norm constraining possible expression shape is counted as grammatical |
| Norm-relative status is compatible with gradience | Token conformity can be determinate even where the grammatical status or scope of the relevant relation is layered, changing, or contested | Every boundary case is rescued by changing the norm centre after the response is known |
| The worldly norm and its measurement can be separated | The paper states what the relation is, what evidence bears on it, and how formal analyses represent it | The profile alternates between being the norm and being a score derived from evidence |
| Semantic/pragmatic theories and grammar answer different questions | The paper shows when semantics is part of the assembled value and when it instead explains consequences of a failure | “Grammar” is assigned only after semantic repair has succeeded or failed |
| The discourse application survives the value correction | At least one case contains an internal linguistic value conflict and a separately supported value-preserving realization error | All “wrong values” are merely false, infelicitous, or unintended licensed choices |
| The paper remains empirically answerable | Norm evidence and response evidence are independently specified, with explicit counterexamples and defeat conditions | The constitutive proposal accommodates every result and therefore licenses no non-trivial inference |

The strongest inversion combines the first two rows: if recurrent assembly, choice
architecture, and scoped licensing cannot distinguish a grammatical norm from a
lexical, phonotactic, representational, or usage norm except by predicting the word
*grammar*, then the philosophical paper has failed. In that event, the honest
fallback is the narrower empirical fault-attribution paper, not a definitional
rescue.

## Revised ontology

The paper should keep five objects separate. The first answers status constitution;
the second applies the constituted norm to a token; the remaining levels concern
consequence, response, and warrant.

| Level | Worldly or epistemic bearer | Question |
|---|---|---|
| Norm architecture | A recurrent form--value assembly relation in a scoped repertoire | What kind of linguistic relation is this, and what makes it grammatical rather than lexical, graphic, situational, or institutional? |
| Token status | A token's conformity to that relation | Does the token have coverage, compatible instructions, supplied obligatory values, and a licensed value and realization? |
| Semantic/pragmatic consequence | The interpreted utterance and communicative act | What happens to composition, reference, force, commitment, continuity, or response possibilities? |
| Public response | A judgment, correction, repair, accommodation, or sanction | What did this participant or institution treat as troublesome, and why? |
| Epistemic access | Corpora, substitutions, elicitation, judgments, repairs, and theoretical models | What warrants the analyst's claims about the other levels? |

Two refinements are essential.

First, formal derivability is model-relative. A formal grammar can represent a
community norm, but derivation does not create that norm and undergeneration does
not prove community ungrammaticality. The paper must distinguish:

- being generated or licensed by a specified formal theory;
- being conventional in the target repertoire;
- a token conforming to the conventional relation;
- a speaker judging or describing the token as grammatical.

Second, the norm ontology must not collapse into frequency or sanction.
Grammatical assembly norms are practice-constituting relations supported by
convergent production and interpretation, counterfactual substitution and omission
patterns, productivity, and reciprocal expectations within a scoped repertoire.
They need not be frequent, explicitly prescribed, morally obligatory, uniformly
represented in every speaker, or institutionally enforced. Frequency, correction,
and sanctions can sustain or reveal a norm without constituting its token-level
status.

The manuscript should claim no stronger world-side commitment than this socially
stable, historically situated organization. It should not call grammar
self-regulating or homeostatic, and it need not show a control mechanism.

### The make-or-break functional distinction

The central argument cannot move directly from recurrence and licensing to
grammatical status. Other linguistic norms recur, constrain tokens, and support
standards of correctness. The paper must individuate them by the role they govern:

| Norm type | Governing role | Diagnostic contrast |
|---|---|---|
| Grammatical assembly | how forms realize or combine semantic, relational, or structural values as instances of recurrent constructions | a selected value, constructional relation, or its licensed realization fails |
| Lexical sign identity and content | which conventional sign is present and which content it contributes | a licensed sign is mis-selected, or its lexical identity is not established |
| Phonological form formation | permissible sound, syllable, prosodic, or word-form shape | expression shape fails without a constructional value relation failing |
| Graphic representation | how an independently identified linguistic expression is represented in writing | spelling or graphic convention fails while linguistic assembly remains intact |
| Usage and social deployment | which licensed expression, repertoire, persona, or act fits an occasion | assembly succeeds but situational or social fit fails |
| Institutional metalinguistic classification | what an institution calls correct grammar | the label can accurately or inaccurately target any of the preceding relations |

Relations may occupy more than one role. The claim is therefore role-relative and
allows layered status; it is not a partition of substances. A phonological pattern
that realizes a tense value can participate in grammatical assembly, whereas pure
phonotactics does not. An item-specific preposition restriction can participate in
both lexical sign selection and a grammatical valence chain. This possibility of
mixed roles must be fixed from the relation's organization rather than introduced
after a response is observed.

The boundary cases are adversarial tests of this distinction, not illustrations
selected to flatter it:

- *le hiver* tests grammatical realization against general phonotactics;
- *goed* tests inflectional value realization against lexeme-specific word form;
- *depend of* tests grammatical complementation against lexical collocation;
- honorifics and register test grammaticalized social values against deployment;
- orthography tests representation against linguistic constitution;
- selectional restrictions test constructional compatibility against lexical
  semantics.

If the functional roles do not yield informative, pre-outcome analyses of these
cases, “assembly norm” remains a promissory label.

### Sorting the profile components

The current profile contains elements with different philosophical roles. The
revision should not preserve them as one undifferentiated worldly bundle.

| Current element | Role in the revised account |
|---|---|
| Form--value assembly at a constructional site | candidate constitutive basis of grammatical status |
| Contrastive choice architecture | organization internal to the norm |
| Conditioned community licensing and reciprocal expectation | social existence and scope of the norm |
| Recurrence, productivity, boundedness, omission, and substitution patterns | evidence for, and sometimes stabilizers of, that organization |
| Formal grammar or constructional analysis | theoretical representation of the relation |
| Grammar labels and corrections | fallible evidence of target attribution and an explanandum |
| Semantic, pragmatic, and discourse effects | consequences of some values or failures, not membership grounds |

The world-side relation has an organization and role. The analyst estimates that
organization from evidence; the estimate carries uncertainty; judgments and
repairs test whether speakers track the relation and how they respond to its
failure. Only the estimate should be called a profile unless the manuscript can
maintain a different terminology without slippage.

## Repairing value failure

Replace the current broad category *wrong value* with three cases:

1. **Internal value incompatibility.** Values supplied by linguistic material in
   the token conflict, as in agreement, temporal concord, or a suitably controlled
   switch-reference dependency. This can be a grammatical fault.
2. **External value misapplication.** A licensed value is successfully expressed
   but does not fit the situation. This is normally semantic or pragmatic, not
   grammatical.
3. **Message-selection error.** A speaker selects a grammatical competitor that
   does not express the intended message. The production can be mistaken without
   the resulting expression being ungrammatical.

The discourse-consequence hypothesis should compare **internal value conflict**
with **value-preserving realization failure**. External misapplication should be a
boundary control rather than a grammatical condition.

The current Amele finite-tense example is not secure as written. An
“unambiguously earlier-today context” makes the yesterday-past form a poor choice,
but not necessarily an ungrammatical one. Retain the example only if Roberts's
co-occurrence restrictions support an overt linguistic conflict, such as a tense
form combined with an incompatible time expression. Otherwise remove it from the
central argument. The switch-reference comparison is more promising, but the
paper must state which linguistically represented subject or continuity values
conflict and must preserve Roberts's cautions about agentivity, clause level,
time, place, world, and pragmatic deixis.

## New structure and disposition of the current material

### 1. The selective-classification problem

- Keep *le hiver* and the sentence “An asterisk records a classification; it
  doesn't yet explain that classification.”
- State the norm-relative thesis within the first two pages.
- Distinguish source cause from fault target, but place both in the broader
  explanatory-level architecture.
- Remove the version note, OVMG, the three-projection roadmap, and the prospective
  research-programme framing.

### 2. Grammaticality and levels of explanation

- Introduce the five-level table and the world/model distinction.
- Define constitutive, functional, semantic/pragmatic, causal, and epistemic
  explanation.
- Add one compact figure:

  `norm architecture -> token status -> semantic/pragmatic consequence -> response`

  Show direct arrows from processing, task, schooling, stigma, and authority to
  response; show evidence as bearing on every level; use a dashed long-run feedback
  arrow from recurrent practice and response to norm architecture.

### 3. The ontology of grammatical norms

- Develop assembly norms, scoped repertoires, reciprocal expectations, and the
  functional distinction among grammatical assembly, sign identity, phonological
  form formation, graphic representation, deployment, and institutional
  metalinguistic classification.
- Separate norm, formal representation, profile, token status, and judgment.
- Use Coseriu for differentiated norm and evaluative domains, Construction Grammar
  for the form--meaning inventory, and Heltoft and Nørgaard-Sørensen for strict
  paradigm architecture.
- Preserve the grammatical-choice profile only as an analyst's evidential
  representation. Rename it if that remains difficult to keep clear in prose.
- Explain lexicalized and construction-sized relations as layered boundary cases,
  not exceptions to an assumed grammar--lexicon dichotomy.

### 4. How tokens fail assembly norms

- Recast coverage, compatibility, saturation, value selection, realization, and
  licensing as token-level questions belonging to this paper.
- Remove all OVMG branding and citations unless a single non-identifying citation
  proves indispensable; the preferred anonymous submission has none.
- Introduce the three-way value distinction above.
- Make *le hiver*, *goed*, *depend of*, concord, spelling, and register do
  contrastive work at this point.

### 5. What semantic and pragmatic explanations explain

- Put Abrusán at the centre of the semantic boundary: type level, locality, and
  shiftability explain why some composition failures are repairable and how they
  are classified.
- Explain that semantics can both constitute the value in an assembly norm and
  explain the consequences of attempted assembly. These are different roles.
- Present Boye and Harder narratively as the principal alternative account of
  grammatical status. This is the central constitutive dispute, not merely a
  contest between predictors: discursive secondary status, strict paradigm
  architecture, and assembly role propose different identity conditions for
  grammatical norms.
- Present the discourse-consequence vector as a plural functional consequence of
  independently identified grammatical choices, with Bary and Simons preventing a
  unitary public-state interpretation.
- Retain the information-theoretic intuition narrowly: constrained alternatives
  can make an error detectable and recoverable; entropy or divergence can measure
  consequences only after linguistic analysis supplies the alternatives.

### 6. Boundary cases and a discourse application

- Rebuild the current boundary table around level independence rather than
  “candidate attribution.”
- Retain French article realization, inflection, complementation, register or
  accent, orthography, and one Amele case.
- Remove the finite-tense case unless the internal incompatibility can be grounded.
- Use switch-reference, if it survives, to illustrate how an internal grammatical
  value conflict can alter reference continuity while a value-preserving
  realization error need not.
- The application may state a repair-locus prediction, but it should no longer be
  one of three coequal contributions.

### 7. Methodological consequences and defeat conditions

Compress the current Study A/Study B machinery by roughly two-thirds. Keep:

- independent specification of the scoped norm;
- the distinction between evidence about the norm and the response to be explained;
- disjoint evidence and outcome partitions where feasible;
- open response before forced labels;
- source-cause rivals such as processing, schooling, stigma, task, and authority;
- semantic repairability and discursive secondary status as theory-level
  alternatives;
- explicit failure conditions.

Cut or reserve for an empirical companion paper:

- Study A/Study B and Stage 1/Stage 2 nomenclature;
- sampling stopping rules and strict-paradigm sensitivity machinery;
- scale weighting and missing-data rules;
- design simulation, minimum-effect, and power specifications;
- long covariate inventories and confirmatory-model formulas;
- detailed eligibility rules for a study not yet being run.

Held-out prediction remains a non-trivial epistemic test. The profile succeeds only
if evidence about norm architecture licenses new expectations about classifications
or responses not used to construct the profile. Prediction does not become part of
the definition of the norm.

### 8. Conclusion

- Answer the initiating question in one concise movement.
- Restate the non-reduction among norm, token status, consequence, response, and
  evidence.
- Name semantic repairability and discourse consequence as partial explanations.
- Mention one empirical consequence and the central defeat condition.
- Do not repeat the protocol, OVMG relation, paper history, or three separate
  research programmes.

## Provisional abstract target

The abstract should remain short and describe a result rather than a planned study:

> Why can an interpretable deviation be grammatically defective in one case but
> instead count as semantic anomaly, poor wording, accent, style, or social
> impropriety in another? I argue that grammaticality is a token's norm-relative
> status within a scoped repertoire of conventional form--value assembly
> relations. This distinguishes the architecture of linguistic norms, a token's
> locus of conformity or failure, the semantic and pragmatic consequences of that
> status, and speakers' judgments, repairs, and sanctions. A grammatical-choice
> profile is evidence about a norm, not the norm itself, and responses are fallible
> evidence rather than truth-makers. Constructional and paradigmatic organization
> can sustain grammatical norms; a token's nonconformity constitutes the fault.
> Semantic repairability and discourse consequence explain important subsets of
> its effects. The account is testable only where
> the relevant norm and the response used to evaluate it are identified
> independently.

This wording is provisional. The ontology and boundary cases must earn the claim
before it becomes the final abstract. The final abstract should remain in the
110--135 word range.

## Clarity and citation specification

The previously approved clarity plan remains in force.

- Write the article from the reader's present; remove version history and
  draft-to-draft commentary.
- Remove repeated defensive assurances once each distinction has been stated in
  its proper section.
- Prefer positive claims over corrective negation except where a live contrast is
  the argument.
- Use a narrative citation when an author, work, or programme is the subject of
  the sentence or supplies the contrast being discussed.
- Use a parenthetical citation when the paper states its own claim and the source
  supplies evidence, background, or converging examples.
- Do not move citations mechanically to sentence beginnings or endings.
- Use author names in prose for Abrusán, Coseriu, Heltoft and
  Nørgaard-Sørensen, Boye and Harder, Bary, and Simons when their accounts are
  doing argumentative work.
- Keep the conclusion short enough that it does not reproduce the introduction.

## Completion condition

The reframing is complete when a reader can answer five questions without knowing
the paper's history or its relationship to OVMG:

1. What worldly relation makes a fault grammatical?
2. What exactly has failed in the token?
3. What semantic or pragmatic consequence follows, if any?
4. Why did a person or institution respond as it did?
5. What evidence warrants each answer?

The paper must also make clear that a formal analysis is a representation, a profile
is an evidential instrument, and a judgment or repair is fallible evidence. If those
separations collapse, the new venue framing has not earned the rewrite.
